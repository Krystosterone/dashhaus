---
title: Why Dashhaus
description: "What Dashhaus was built to fix about Home Assistant dashboards, and the choices that follow from it."
---

Home Assistant's own dashboards are built from cards that each know Home Assistant: how to read an entity, which
service to call, how to draw it. A new look means rewriting all three, card by card, and a wall panel, a phone and a
tablet each tend to end up with a dashboard of their own, maintained by hand.

Dashhaus splits those three apart, so each is written once.

## One model per entity, drawn many ways

Reading Home Assistant is written once per kind of entity: a mapper turns the raw state into a plain model
(`LightEntity`, `ClimateEntity`), an action module turns a tap into the right service call, and a `useHa<Name>` hook
hands both to whatever draws. Orb and Field are two drawings of the same ten models; neither contains a line of Home
Assistant code. A third skin, or your own app, gets the same models and the same safety checks (an unavailable
entity, a value out of range, a service Home Assistant refuses) without writing them.

## Dashboards that hold still

Each skin has rules a card can't break, held by tests: a card's height never changes with its state, so a dashboard
doesn't reflow when a light turns on; nothing animates layout; colour is never the only signal; an unavailable device
looks unavailable, never off. More detail opens in a sheet or a panel instead of growing the card. These are why the
same dashboard reads on a phone in the hand and on a wall panel across the room.

## Laid out by Home Assistant, edited in Home Assistant

A skin is one Lovelace card. With no layout written, it groups your house by area from Home Assistant's registries and
keeps up as you add devices. When you want your own order, you write YAML in Home Assistant's code editor; the preview
redraws as you type and every mistake is listed at once, with its path and the probable fix.

## Checked in a browser, not in production

Every card, in every state and footprint, is a Storybook story on demo data, and every story must render and pass an
accessibility check before a change lands. The live dashboards on [Orb and Field](/dashhaus/skins/) are those stories.

## No credentials in the bundle

In Home Assistant, a skin uses the frontend's own authenticated connection: the bundle carries no token. The deploy
tool's token stays on the computer you deploy from. In an app of your own, login is yours too; the packages never
store or refresh a token ([Connecting and auth](/dashhaus/errors/auth/)).
