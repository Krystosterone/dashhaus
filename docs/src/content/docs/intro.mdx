Dashhaus draws Home Assistant dashboards in React and TypeScript, for phones, tablets and wall panels. It is two things:

- **Two finished skins**, [Orb](/dashhaus/orb/) and [Field](/dashhaus/field/). Each is a whole look for a dashboard,
  installed on its own from the Marketplace and laid out from your house, or in YAML. Nothing to build.
- **The headless packages under them.** The entity models, the Home Assistant store, a `useHa<Name>` hook and a
  headless component for each kind of entity, the interaction primitives and the card frame. A skin is drawing on top
  of these, so you can draw your own: in a React app of yours, or as a new skin with its own Lovelace card.

## Beta

Dashhaus is in beta: the latest release is <Version />. It hasn't been tried on many homes yet, so it can still change
quickly.

- **An update can ask something of you.** An update may rename a YAML key, change a hook or redraw a card. Each
  [release](https://github.com/Krystosterone/dashhaus/releases) says what changed and what to do; read it before you
  update.
- **Your existing dashboards are safe.** Adding the Dashhaus dashboard and the deploy command only create dashboards;
  they never change one that exists. The ones they create follow Dashhaus as it updates.
- **Real devices vary.** Every card is tested on Home Assistant's standard entities, but an integration may report its
  device in a way no card expects yet. Locks, alarm panels and covers act on real things: try each one once while you
  can see it.
- **In your own code**, beta versions are `0.x`, and a change that breaks something moves the middle number (0.2 to
  0.3). A caret range stays on one `0.x` line, so moving to the next is a step you take. Every `@dashhaus/` package
  shares one version, so move them together. 1.0 will be the first stable release.

Testers are welcome. Put it on your house, a phone or a wall panel, and
[say how it went](https://github.com/Krystosterone/dashhaus/issues/new/choose): a card that draws wrong, a device it
misreads or leaves out, or simply that it works on your setup. Your Home Assistant and Dashhaus versions and a
screenshot help most.

Deploying from the command line serves the first ways in: a [scripted deploy](/dashhaus/start/deploy/) of Orb or
Field, with a dashboard of your own rows, and the last step of building a skin.

## How the pieces stack

```text
Home Assistant
  └─ a transport: Home Assistant's own connection in a Lovelace card, or one your app opens
       └─ the store (createHaStore) and HaProvider: every entity, the connection status
            └─ useHa<Name>(entityId): the mapped entity, today's history and its actions
                 ├─ your own markup
                 └─ a skin: Orb's <Name>Tile, Field's <Name>Module
                      └─ its Lovelace card: custom:dashhaus-orb, custom:dashhaus-field
```

Each layer only knows the one above it. The entity models never see Home Assistant's raw state; the hooks never draw;
a skin's cards never talk to Home Assistant, they take the hook's result as props.

## Words

These pages use one word per thing, the same as the code and Storybook.

| Word | Means |
| --- | --- |
| Card | One entity drawn on a dashboard. |
| Tile | Orb's card ready for a dashboard: the card in its size container with its details sheet (`LightTile`). |
| Module | Field's card: a plate with its key, screen and fader, carrying its own detail panel (`LightModule`). |
| Details | What a card opens from its name or a press and hold: Orb's sheet, Field's panel. |
| Footprint | A card's fixed height: `compact`, `standard`, `featured` in Orb; `compact`, `standard` in Field. |
| Connected | A card bound to the store: `HaLightTile` in Orb, `HaLightModule` in Field, on the shared `useHaLight` hook. |
| Lovelace card | The custom element Home Assistant loads (`custom:dashhaus-orb`), which draws a whole dashboard from its YAML. |
| Primitive | A headless interaction hook (`usePress`, `useSlider`): behaviour, no markup. |
| Part | A skin's styled control built on a primitive: Orb's `TogglePill`, Field's `Key`. |
| Dashboard | A page of cards and what frames them: the header, rows of cards and the nav bar. |
