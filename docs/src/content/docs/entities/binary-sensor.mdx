One `binary_sensor.*` entity, with the battery sensor on its device: a door or a window open or closed, a leak sensor
wet or dry, a smoke, gas or carbon monoxide detector clear or not, and when it last changed. Read-only.

## Quick example

<Example file="entities/binarySensor/DoorState.tsx" />

Home Assistant reports every binary sensor as `on` or `off`; its `device_class` says what that means. `stateWord` says
it in the class's words: a door `on` is Open, a leak sensor's Wet, a smoke detector's Smoke. A sensor Home Assistant
can't reach is `unavailable`, never a safe-looking Closed or Clear.

## Component

`<HaBinarySensor>` is `useHaBinarySensor` as a component that draws nothing of its own. It takes every option of the
hook as a prop, and hands the hook's result to its child function. Use it where hooks can't go, such as one per item
of a list.

## Hook

It reads the sensor, its area, given `batteryEntityId` the battery sensor on the same device, and today's history of
when it was on.

## What needs looking at

`binarySensorSeverity` ranks what a sensor's state may claim, the same in every skin: smoke, gas and carbon monoxide
are an alarm, a leak urgent, a garage door left open and a low battery advisory. `alertText` says the warning in a few
words (`Smoke alarm`, `Open since 18:30`), and `alertSaid` says it whole for a screen reader without the card's name,
so a skin can put the name first. An open door is a state, not a warning, however long it lasts.

## In a skin

Orb's `HaBinarySensorTile` and Field's `HaBinarySensorModule` are `useHaBinarySensor` above each skin's card. Each
draws the picture its device class names and says the state in its words. Smoke sounds the card's alarm (Orb fills
its slot, Field inverts its screen), and a leak or a garage door left open is marked as a warning, never a glow. They
take the hook's options as props, plus their own:
