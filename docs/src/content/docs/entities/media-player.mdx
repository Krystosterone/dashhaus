One `media_player.*` entity, a speaker, a TV or a cast: whether it is playing, paused, buffering, idle, on, in standby
or off; what is playing and how far in; its volume, whether it is muted, and its source; its artwork; and what it can
do, from what it declares to Home Assistant.

Integrations name the same state differently: an older one says `stopped` where Home Assistant says `idle`, another
`loading` for buffering. They are mapped onto Home Assistant's own, and any other state is said in its own words,
`Connecting`, never guessed. A stream, such as a radio station, has no duration: nothing is drawn for how far in,
rather than a guess.

## How far in, without a timer

Home Assistant reports a position and when it read it, not a ticking clock. `usePlayhead`, from
`@dashhaus/core/hooks`, works out where the player is from those, once, and again only when the position, the state or
the track changes; what you draw from it can then move on by itself with CSS, so nothing re-renders every second.
`clockPlaces` gives a clock's places, each a digit that turns every so many seconds, for a clock that keeps time the
same way.

## Seeking

A player that declares it can seek (`supportsSeek`) moves to a position you give it in seconds: `actions.onSeek`,
which answers `true` once Home Assistant has taken it and `false` when it didn't. Each seek makes most players
rebuffer, so seek once, when the finger lifts, not on every move. `useSeek`, from `@dashhaus/core/hooks`, does that:
while a finger or a key is on the bar it holds a draft you draw, still; it seeks once on release; and the bar runs on
from where it was let go until Home Assistant reports the new position, with no snap back. Its `slider` is what
`useSlider` takes, from `@dashhaus/core/primitives`, whose `onCommit` is called once when a gesture ends.

## Component

`<HaMediaPlayer>` is `useHaMediaPlayer` as a component that draws nothing of its own. It takes every option of the hook
as a prop, and hands the hook's result to its child function. Use it where hooks can't go, such as one per item of a
list.

### Returns

#### `entity`: MediaPlayerEntity

Each `supports…` flag comes from what the player declares to Home Assistant. What is playing, the volume and the source
are known only while it is available. `picture` is its artwork, a URL your page can load, while something is loaded:
draw it with `usePicture`, which loads it only once it is on screen.

<ApiMembers from="@dashhaus/core/entities/mediaPlayer/types" name="MediaPlayerEntity" />

#### `history`: MediaPlayerHistory

Today, from Home Assistant's recorder, kept up to date by every change since: the hours it was playing. Pass `history`
to supply your own, or `loadHistory={false}` to skip the read.

<ApiMembers from="@dashhaus/core/entities/mediaPlayer/types" name="MediaPlayerHistory" />

#### `actions`: MediaPlayerHandlers

<ApiMembers from="@dashhaus/core" name="MediaPlayerHandlers" />

An action the player doesn't declare, or any action while it is unavailable, is reported to `onActionError` as
`unsupported` and never sent; a source it doesn't list is reported as `invalid_input`.

## In a skin

Orb's `HaMediaPlayerTile` and Field's `HaMediaPlayerModule` are `useHaMediaPlayer` above each skin's card. One press
pauses it while it plays, plays it while it doesn't and turns it on while it is off; skipping, seeking (where the
player can), the volume, mute, the source and turning it off are in its details, never on the dashboard, where a stray
tap would jump the track. Orb seeks along a groove in its sheet, Field along a Position fader in its panel. Its artwork is shown where there is room, never in a row. On a dashboard
set `quiet`, where nothing changes by itself, they draw no line or clock for how far in. They take the hook's options
as props, plus their own:
