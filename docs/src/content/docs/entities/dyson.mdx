One Dyson purifier fan, as the [ha-dyson](https://github.com/libdyson-wg/ha-dyson) custom integration (`dyson_local`)
adds it: a Pure Cool, a Hot+Cool, a Humidify+Cool or a Pure Cool Link. Its `fan.*` entity is the fan card's, with the
entities on its device beside it: the air it measures, its filters' life, its night mode and, on a Hot+Cool, its heater.

## Quick example

<Example file="entities/dyson/Air.tsx" />

Its ten speeds are a percentage, as any fan's, but Dyson's presets are only Auto and Normal, so the model says `auto`
instead. In Auto the fan chooses its own speed and Home Assistant reports none; setting a speed leaves Auto.

Each reading is banded as Dyson's app bands it: Good, Fair, Poor and Very poor. Particles are in µg/m³, VOC and NO₂ on
Dyson's 0-10 index, formaldehyde in mg/m³. The air is as poor as its worst reading. Poor air, or a filter at 10% or
under, is a warning, and the air comes first.

## Hook

<ApiSignature from="@dashhaus/core" name="useHaDyson" />

It reads the fan, the entities `dyson_local` names after it, its area and today's history. A fan called `fan.bedroom`
has its fine particles in `sensor.bedroom_pm_2_5`, its night mode in `switch.bedroom_night_mode` and its heater in
`climate.bedroom`. Any of these that isn't there is left out.

## Tasks

### Oscillation, night mode and heat

<Example file="entities/dyson/Extras.tsx" />

A Pure Cool sweeps 45°, 90°, 180° or 350° around where it points now, set with `dyson_local.set_angle`. A
Humidify+Cool lists its own ranges, Breeze among them, on its oscillation mode select. A Hot+Cool's heat is its
heater's `heat` mode. Turned off, the heater goes to `cool`, and the fan keeps running.

## In a skin

Orb's `HaDysonTile` and Field's `HaDysonModule` are `useHaDyson` above each skin's card. Both set the speed on the fan's
groove or fader, which says Auto while the fan chooses its own speed. Mode, oscillation range, airflow, night mode, heat,
every reading and the filters are in its details. A Lovelace card draws one with `card: dyson`. The automatic layouts
find a Dyson themselves by the night-mode switch and filter sensor named after its fan. They take the hook's options as
props, plus their own:
