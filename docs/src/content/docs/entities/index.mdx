Each kind of entity has the same four things: a hook and a headless component in `@dashhaus/core`, a tile in
`@dashhaus/orb` and a module in `@dashhaus/field`. The hook is the one implementation; the other three are it, drawn
or not.

| [Toggle](/dashhaus/entities/toggle/) | `input_boolean.*`, `switch.*` | `useHaToggle` | `HaToggle` | `HaToggleTile` | `HaToggleModule` |
| [Cover](/dashhaus/entities/cover/) | `cover.*` | `useHaCover` | `HaCover` | `HaCoverTile` | `HaCoverModule` |

## What every hook returns

`useHa<Name>(entityId, options)` returns:

- `entity`: the normalized model. It is always there, even before Home Assistant has delivered the entity or while it
  is unavailable: its `state` is then `"unavailable"`, and its fields are what's known.
- `history`: today, hour by hour, from Home Assistant's recorder and every change since. A lock has none: it shows
  its last change.
- `actions`: what the entity can do, for a control. An optional handler (a light's `onKelvinChange`, a cover's
  `onSetPosition`) is left out when the entity can't use it, so test the handler before drawing its control. Sensors
  and the weather have no actions; the weather adds `hourly` and `daily` forecasts.

Every hook takes `name` and `area` to override what Home Assistant says, `history` and `loadHistory` to supply or skip
today's read, and, for a control, `onActionError` ([Action errors](/dashhaus/errors/actions/)).

## Calls are a request, not a result

An action sends one Home Assistant service call and returns. The entity changes when Home Assistant reports that it
changed, through the store, never before: a light shows on because it is on. So a control drawn on a hook is never
optimistic, and a call Home Assistant refuses never leaves the screen out of step.

An entity id from the wrong domain, or one that isn't an entity id at all, gets a console warning in development
naming the hook. It is almost always a typo, and otherwise the card would only ever say unavailable.
