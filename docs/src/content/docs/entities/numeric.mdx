One `sensor.*` entity with a number: CO₂ in ppm, power in W, energy in kWh, or anything else a sensor measures. One
card for all of them: its device class and its unit choose its words, its icon and how its figure is written.
Read-only.

## Quick example

<Example file="entities/numeric/Co2Reading.tsx" />

A sensor's domain says nothing of what it measures, so the card reads its `device_class`. `numericWords` names it,
short for a line (`CO₂`, `Power`) and long for small print (`Carbon dioxide`). `numericFigure` writes the figure in five
places at most: `650`, `21.4`, `0.42`, and beyond five, scaled (`123k`, `1.2M`), so a card never cuts a digit.
`withUnit` puts a symbol on the figure (`41%`) and a word after a space (`650 ppm`). A sensor Home Assistant can't reach
is `unavailable`, and a state that isn't a number is no reading, never a made-up value.

The `sensor` card stays the default for every `sensor.*`. On a dashboard, pick this one with `card: numeric`. A
dashboard laid out for you uses it for the room's air quality only (CO₂, particles, VOC), one per device. Add power,
energy and any other reading yourself, since every smart plug has them.

## Component

`<HaNumeric>` is `useHaNumeric` as a component that draws nothing of its own. It takes every option of the hook as a
prop, and hands the hook's result to its child function. Use it where hooks can't go, such as one per item of a list.

## Today

`numericToday` says the day the same way in every skin. A measurement gives its low and high. A meter that only climbs
(`state_class: total_increasing`, such as energy or water) gives how far it rose since the day's first reading, since
its low and high would only be midnight and now.

## In a skin

Orb's `HaNumericTile` and Field's `HaNumericModule` are `useHaNumeric` above each skin's card. Each draws the picture
its device class names (air, power, a meter or a gauge), the figure in its unit, and today. Neither warns: a CO₂ or
power threshold of the card's own would be a guess. They take the hook's options as props, plus their own:
