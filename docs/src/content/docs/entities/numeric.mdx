One `sensor.*` entity with a number: CO₂ in ppm, power in W, energy in kWh, or anything else a sensor measures. One
card for all of them: its device class and its unit choose its words, its icon and how its figure is written.
Read-only.

## Quick example

<Example file="entities/numeric/Co2Reading.tsx" />

A sensor's domain says nothing of what it measures, so the card reads its `device_class`. `numericWords` names it,
short for a line (`CO₂`, `Power`) and long for small print (`Carbon dioxide`). `numericFigure` writes the figure in five
places at most: `650`, `21.4`, `0.42`, and beyond five, scaled (`123k`, `1.2M`), so a card never cuts a digit.
`withUnit` puts a symbol on the figure (`41%`) and a word after a space (`650 ppm`). A sensor Home Assistant can't reach
is `unavailable`, and a state that isn't a number is no reading, never a made-up value.

The `sensor` card stays the default for every `sensor.*`. On a dashboard, pick this one with `card: numeric`. A
dashboard laid out for you uses it for the room's air quality only (CO₂, particles, VOC), one per device. Add power,
energy and any other reading yourself, since every smart plug has them.

## A warning you set

The card never decides on its own that a number is too high: a CO₂ or power limit of its own would be a guess. Set
`warn_above` or `warn_below`, in the sensor's own unit, and the card says when the reading crosses it. Set both for
a band, `warn_below` under `warn_above`. Orb and Field take the same keys.

<Example file="entities/numeric/warnings.yaml" />

Past a limit, the card says so in place of what it measures, behind a warning sign: `High CO₂`, `Low voltage`,
`Too cold` for a temperature, and `Too high` or `Too low` where its kind has no short word. A screen reader hears
"Warning: Carbon dioxide above 1200 ppm". A reading at the limit doesn't warn. The details always say the limits you
set ("Warns above 1200 ppm"), so you can check why it warns. In React they are `useHaNumeric`'s `warnAbove` and
`warnBelow`, and `numericWarning` says whether a reading is past one.

It is a look-when-convenient warning, never an alarm: the figure keeps its colour, nothing glows and Field's screen
never turns light. The card shows it only while the reading is past the limit, with no delay or hysteresis, so a
reading that wavers around it comes and goes. For a warning that must reach you (a phone notification, an automation,
a freezer that has failed), make a **Threshold** helper in Home Assistant (Settings → Devices & services → Helpers)
and automate on it. Its binary sensor can sit on the dashboard too, beside the reading.

prop, and hands the hook's result to its child function. Use it where hooks can't go, such as one per item of a list.

## Today

`numericToday` says the day the same way in every skin. A measurement gives its low and high. A meter that only climbs
(`state_class: total_increasing`, such as energy or water) gives how far it rose since the day's first reading, since
its low and high would only be midnight and now.

## In a skin

Orb's `HaNumericTile` and Field's `HaNumericModule` are `useHaNumeric` above each skin's card. Each draws the picture
its device class names (air, power, a meter or a gauge), the figure in its unit, and today. Neither warns unless you
set a limit (`warnAbove`, `warnBelow`). They take the hook's options as props, plus their own:
