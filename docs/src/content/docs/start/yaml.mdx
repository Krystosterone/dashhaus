Each skin is one Lovelace card: `custom:dashhaus-orb` for Orb, `custom:dashhaus-field` for Field. The card draws the
whole dashboard from its YAML.

## The automatic layout

With nothing but its `type`, the card lays out every supported entity it can see, a row per area from Home
Assistant's registries, and keeps up as you add devices. The dashboards the deploy tool creates start this way. Hidden,
configuration and diagnostic entities are left out; room sensors are paired with the humidity and battery sensors on the same device.

## Your own layout

Open the dashboard, **⋮ → Edit dashboard**, the card's pencil, then **Show code editor**. The preview redraws as you
type, and every mistake is listed at once, with where it is and what you probably meant:

```text
Dashhaus config has 2 problems:
• rows[1].cards[0] (sensor.bedroom_temperature): unknown key "humdity" — did you mean "humidity"?
• rows[2].cards[3]: media_player.tv: media_player.* entities have no card yet
```

The simplest layout is a list of `cards`, drawn in that order on one grid. `columns` sets how many fit across; leave
it out and the card fits as many as the screen holds. Add, remove or move a line and the page follows:

<Example file="user/cards.yaml" />

For headings, write `rows` instead: one under another, each with its own `title`, `columns` and `cards`. A row is
flat: it holds cards, never other rows. Each card can say where it sits in its row:

- `width`: how many columns it spans, or `full` for all of them. Never pixels.
- `column`: the column it starts in. Cards given the same column stack down it.
- `footprint`: `compact`, `standard` or (Orb) `featured`: the card's fixed height.

And each row how it packs them:

- `flow: dense` lets a later card fill an earlier gap; the default keeps your order on screen.
- `align: bottom` sets a row's shorter columns on its foot: a sidebar beside a tall hero ends where the hero does.

On a narrower screen a row keeps its cards and their heights and takes fewer columns: widths shrink to fit, and once
a row has fewer columns than it was written for, its cards flow in your order instead of keeping their `column`. A
row narrowed by its surroundings does the same. A state change never moves a card.

## When the YAML is wrong

The card checks the whole YAML before it draws, and lists every problem at once: where it is, what's wrong, and what to
write instead. This Orb card has five of the mistakes people make most:

<Example file="mistakes/orb.yaml" />

```text
Dashhaus config has 5 problems:
• "cards" and "rows" both lay out this page: keep "cards" for one plain grid, or move those cards into a row under "rows"
• rows[0] (climate.living_room): this is a card, not a row: put it in a row's "cards", or list the cards under "cards" at the top for one plain grid
• rows[1].cards[0] (climate.living_room): "width: 3" is wider than this row's 2 columns: use width: full, or raise the row's columns to 3
• rows[1].cards[1] (light.living_room): "span" isn't a key: a card spans columns with "width", a whole number or full
• rows[2]: "columns" must be a whole number from 1 to 12, or auto — write columns: 3, without quotes
```

- **`cards` and `rows` together.** A page is one or the other: `cards` for one plain grid, `rows` for titled groups.
  Move the loose cards into a row of their own.
- **A card where a row belongs.** Every entry under `rows` is a map with its own `cards`; an entity id there is a
  card. Indent it under a row's `cards`.
- **A card wider than its row.** `width` counts the row's columns, so it can't be more than the row's `columns`. Use
  `width: full` for all of them.
- **`span`.** A card's columns are `width`.
- **A number in quotes.** `"3"` is text to YAML; write `columns: 3`.

In Field, a row holds one footprint, so a compact module beside standard ones needs either a row of its own or a
`column` on every module of the row, which stacks them by column:

<Example file="mistakes/field.yaml" />

```text
Dashhaus config has 2 problems:
• rows[0].cards[0] (light.ceiling): this row mixes standard and compact modules, so each stacks in a column: give it a "column", or move the compact ones into a row of their own
• rows[0].cards[1] (cover.living_room): this row mixes standard and compact modules, so each stacks in a column: give it a "column", or move the compact ones into a row of their own
```

Home Assistant reads the YAML before the card does, so it reports a broken file itself (a bad indent, a tab, an
unclosed quote) and the card never sees it. A mistake that is still valid YAML, such as a missing `- ` that turns a
list into one map, reaches the card as whatever it parsed to.

## Which card draws an entity

The entity's domain picks its card. Where two cards draw one domain, `card:` picks the other:

| Entity | Card |
| --- | --- |
| `light.*` | light |
| `climate.*` | climate |
| `sensor.*` | sensor, with its device's humidity and battery sensors |
| `sensor.*_sleep_state` | owlet, an Owlet sock; `card: owlet` for a sock named otherwise |
| `weather.*` | weather, with its air-quality sensor and `sun.sun` |
| `cover.*` | cover |
| `input_boolean.*`, `switch.*` | toggle |
| `switch.*` with `card: sound` | sound, with its volume `number.*` found beside it |
| `alarm_control_panel.*` | alarm |
| `humidifier.*` | humidifier |
| `lock.*` | lock, with its device's battery sensor |
| `fan.*` | fan |

An entity with no card is an error that says so, rather than an empty space.

## Keys

| Where | Orb | Field |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The card | `title`, `subtitle`, `show_time`, `hour_cycle`, `viewing`, `theme`, `accent`, `pages`, `nav` | `title`, `subtitle`, `show_time`, `hour_cycle`, `viewing`, `finish`, `footprint`, `quiet`, `pages`, `nav` |
| The page (the card, or one of its `pages`) | `cards` or `rows`; `columns` (1 to 12, or `auto`) and `flow` for every row | `cards` or `rows`; `columns` (1 to 4, or `auto`) and `flow` for every row |
| A row | `title`, `note`, `columns`, `flow`, `align`, `cards` | `title`, `note`, `columns`, `flow`, `align`, `cards` |
| Any card | `entity`, `card`, `name`, `area`, `footprint`, `width`, `column`, `show_control`, `history` | `entity`, `card`, `name`, `area`, `footprint`, `width`, `column`, `history` |
| A bundle | `bundle` (`joined`, `lead`, `titled`), `cards`, `label`, `sheet_title` (lead), `title` and `area` (titled), `width`, `column` | none |

Each card adds its own: a sensor's `humidity`, `battery`, `outdoor` and `unit`; the weather's `aqi`, `aqi_scale`, `sun`
and `forecasts`; a toggle's `icon` and `description`; a sound's `volume`; an alarm's `read_only`, `exit_delay`,
`entry_delay` and `open_sensors`; an Owlet sock's `heart_rate`, `oxygen`, `charging`, `base_station`, `alerts` and
`stale_after`; a lock's `battery`; a climate entity's `unit`. In Orb, most cards also take a `color` for their orb.

`viewing: far` is for a wall panel read from across the room: larger status lines and captions, heavier figures,
the same card heights. A key Orb takes and Field doesn't (`theme`, `accent`, `color`, `show_control`, bundles) is
refused with the reason, for YAML copied from one card to the other.

The specification's [deploy page](https://github.com/Krystosterone/dashhaus/blob/main/spec/deploy.md#the-format)
has every value and default.

## Pages and the nav

A nav docks at the bottom of the screen, one pill or key per destination. Without `nav`, it lists the card's pages,
or else its titled rows. `pages` are more pages in the same card, each with its own `cards` or `rows`; the URL keeps
the page open (`#page=nursery`), so the back button returns.

<Example file="user/pages.yaml" />

A destination is a `section:` of the main page, a titled row by its title, a `page:` by its `id` (`home` is the main page), or
`navigate:` to another Home Assistant dashboard by its path. `nav: false` turns the nav off; a wall panel has none
unless `nav` lists one.

Both skins take the same `pages` and `nav`.
