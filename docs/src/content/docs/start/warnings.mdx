Both skins show a problem on the card it belongs to, in words, never by colour alone, so it still reads with colour
turned off or to a screen reader. How loud it gets depends on how soon you need to act: a low battery waits until it
suits you, a baby's low oxygen doesn't. A card never moves or changes height because of a problem, so you find it
where you always find that device.

## What counts as a problem

Each card knows its own, from what Home Assistant reports:

| Card | Look when convenient | Act soon | Act now |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sensor | battery at 15% or less | | |
| Weather | poor air (US AQI over 100, with `aqi_scale: US`), gusts of 50 km/h or more, UV of 8 or more | | |
| Cover | a garage door, door or gate left open | | |
| Alarm | disarmed with doors or windows open (`open_sensors`) | the entry delay, counting down | triggered |
| Lock | battery at 15% or less | jammed | |
| Owlet | the sock's battery low | the sock off or disconnected, the base station without power, the battery critical, no new reading for `stale_after` minutes (3 by default) | a vitals alert: critical, low or high oxygen; low or high heart rate |

An Owlet base station switched off is a choice, not a fault: both skins say so in plain words, with no warning. An
unlocked door is a state, however long it lasts. The alert sensors and their names are on the
[Owlet page](/dashhaus/entities/owlet/), and each card's keys on [Lay it out in YAML](/dashhaus/start/yaml/#keys).

## In Orb

- **A warning leads the card's status line** in orange behind a warning mark, a small circled exclamation point, and
  a screen reader hears it as a warning: "Battery 12%", "Jammed · may not be locked", "Open since 10:20". Where a card
  has several, the most urgent leads with a count of the rest ("Hazardous air +2"), and its sheet names every one.
  Compact rows keep the mark.
- **Only an alarm that has gone off fills.** A triggered alarm's pill fills orange and pulses gently; an Owlet vitals
  alert fills the card's control the same way and names every alert sounding, the most urgent first, with its figure:
  "Low oxygen · 84% O₂". Under reduced motion they hold still.
- **A problem never glows.** Orb's glow and yellow mean on; a problem gets words and the mark, never more brightness,
  so it never looks like a lamp switched on.

## In Field

Field has no warning colour. Its screens are always dark, so it shows how urgent a problem is by how much of the
screen turns light, which you notice before you read anything, in both finishes:

- **Look when convenient:** an outlined warning sign before its words. A battery's is printed on the plate in place
  of the area; a reading's stays on the screen.
- **Act soon:** the words in a light block on the screen's first line (`Sock off`, `Jammed`); the reading stays.
- **Act now:** the whole screen turns light and its figure becomes the alarm (`79% O₂`, `ALARM`), a rule lines the
  module's edge and its light blinks. It flashes once a second for the first ten seconds, then holds until it clears.
  A module drawn later, or a reconnect, picks up where the flash would be instead of starting it again.

**The alert line** sits under the dashboard's title whenever something needs acting on soon or now, so a problem
below the fold still shows on a phone's first screen. Alarms come first, then the newest; three show, then
"+N more". Tap one to open that module's panel. With nothing pressing, there's no line.

Under reduced motion an alarm holds its light screen without flashing. So does a dashboard set `quiet: true`, for a
nursery's panel at night.

## What a dashboard can't do

A dashboard shows a problem only while it's open, and only as fast as Home Assistant hears of it. Neither skin sends
a notification or makes a sound. For that, use a Home Assistant automation that notifies your phone. For a baby
monitor in particular, Owlet's own app and base station are the alarm; the card repeats what they raised.
