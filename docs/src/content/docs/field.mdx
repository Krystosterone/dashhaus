## How it looks

Each module is a plate with a key you press, a fader you slide and an always-dark screen you read, with printed labels
in capitals. A small orange mark shows what a control sets. Field opens more in a panel from the module's name.

It comes in two finishes, light grey Alloy and Black, and follows Home Assistant's light or dark mode by default. It
reads the same on a phone in your hand and a wall panel across the room: a module never changes height when its state
changes. The tablet below is live, on a demo house.

## Settings

Open the dashboard and press **Edit dashboard** (the pencil, or **⋮**). Its dialog opens on the dashboard's
configuration in YAML; add to it and save:

<Example file="install/field-dashboard.yaml" title="A wall panel, in Black" />

| Key | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `viewing` | `near` (default): a phone or tablet in the hand. `far`: a wall panel read from across the room. |
| `finish` | `home-assistant` (default): Alloy in Home Assistant's light mode, Black in its dark mode. `auto` follows the device; `alloy` and `black` hold one. |
| `layout` | `wall`: the house one column per room, for a panel on a wall (below). Left out, a row per room. |
| `title` | The title the page prints in its header. `Home` by default. |

To lay out your own cards and rows instead, take control of the dashboard (the same dialog's **⋮ → Take control**),
which keeps Field's card as it is now, and edit it; [Lay it out in YAML](/dashhaus/start/yaml/) shows how. A dashboard
you take control of no longer lays itself out.

From a terminal or CI, `@dashhaus/field` and the deploy tool create Field's dashboard and one of your own rows in one
command: [Deploy from the command line](/dashhaus/start/deploy/). Pick one way: each loads its own copy of Field.

## A dashboard for each device

A phone in the hand and a panel on the wall want different pages, so give each its own dashboard:

1. **Settings → Dashboards → Add dashboard → Field** twice: one for the phones, one for the wall. Name them so you can
   tell them apart, like Home and Wall.
2. On the wall's, press **Edit dashboard** and add `layout: wall` and `viewing: far`:

   <Example file="install/field-wall-dashboard.yaml" title="A wall panel, one column per room" />

3. On each device, open your profile (your name at the foot of the sidebar) and pick its dashboard under
   **Dashboard**. Home Assistant keeps that choice per browser, so the wall panel opens on Wall and each phone on Home.

`layout: wall` arranges the whole house, room first: a column for each Home Assistant area that has a device, in the
order Home Assistant keeps your areas in, wrapping onto further rows when there are more rooms than fit across. Field puts as many rooms side by side as the panel's width holds with the name of every key in a room's column whole from across the room: three on a 1280 px panel, four from about 1430 px; each column leads with what you read (a door, a camera, the weather) and puts the keys you press in rows under it, and the room's name is its legend, so a plate under it doesn't repeat it. The devices
in no area (the alarm, the calendars, the guest Wi-Fi code) end the first row, the weather and the sun join an area
called Outside if you have one, and your energy meters are a row of their own at the end. Anything urgent, a jammed lock or a sock's alarm, still shows in the alert line under the header, even when its room is further down the wall.

To change which rooms come first, put them in order in Home Assistant, under **Settings → Areas, labels & zones**:
drag the areas, or use **Reorder** in a floor's **⋮** menu, which orders the floors too. The wall takes each floor's
rooms in turn, then the rooms on no floor, and follows a new order the next time it draws, still following the house.
Until you reorder them, Home Assistant keeps areas in the order you made them.

To change what is in the rooms, take control of the wall's dashboard (**⋮ → Take control**)
and write its rows in place of `layout: wall`: one untitled row, each card given its `column`
([Lay it out in YAML](/dashhaus/start/yaml/) shows how). A wall with rows of its own no longer follows the house. A
`nav` of `navigate:` links lets the wall and the phone dashboards open each other; a dashboard on the strategy can't
link to another until you take control of it. From the command line, [Deploy from the command line](/dashhaus/start/deploy/) creates both, linked.

## If something goes wrong

If **Add dashboard** has no **Field** under Community dashboards, or the dashboard says `Custom element doesn't exist`
or `Timeout waiting for strategy element`, Home Assistant hasn't loaded Field yet. Reload the tab. If that doesn't help,
open **Settings → Dashboards → ⋮ → Resources** and check there's a JavaScript module at
`/hacsfiles/field-dashboard/field-dashboard.js`.

Community dashboards are listed from Home Assistant 2026.5. On an older version, or one that keeps its dashboards in
YAML, create a dashboard yourself and give it `strategy: { type: custom:field-dashboard }` as its whole configuration.

## Update

The Marketplace (or HACS) tells you when there's a new version of Field. Update it there, then reload the tab. Your
dashboard follows; [Release notes](/dashhaus/releases/) say what changed and anything to do first.

## Ask an assistant to do it

An assistant that can use your browser can follow this page on its own. Give it the page's address, or this:

```text
Install the Field dashboard in my Home Assistant. I'm signed in as an administrator.
1. Open https://my.home-assistant.io/redirect/hacs_repository/?owner=Krystosterone&repository=field-dashboard&category=plugin
   and download Field (type: Dashboard; add it as a custom repository if asked). Keep the defaults.
2. Reload the browser tab.
3. Settings → Dashboards → Add dashboard → under Community dashboards, pick "Field" → Create.
4. Open the new Field dashboard from the sidebar and check it draws.
   Don't change any other dashboard, and never ask me for a password or token.
```
