# Field skin

A second skin, `src/skins/field/`, drawn after Teenage Engineering's EP–133 K.O. II: a flat light-grey body, printed labels, flat-topped keys, a thin fader with a round thumb, and a dark segment-style screen. It sits on the same entity models and Home Assistant hooks as orb (`useHaLight` and the rest); only the drawing differs. Stories are under **Field** in Storybook, in the same groups as orb's except Bundles, which are orb's alone, with a **Finish** toolbar (Auto, Alloy, Black). Foundations → Design rules draws this file with live stories beside its sections (edit it here, not there), Foundations → Palette shows the parts below and what each means, Foundations → Parts each part across its states, and Dashboards → Lovelace card is its whole dashboard from YAML.

Status: light, climate, sensor, weather, toggle, humidifier, sound, cover, alarm and owlet are implemented, each with a detail panel; sensor, weather and owlet are readings, the pattern the other read-only cards follow, and toggle is the plainest control, one key. The Lovelace card, `custom:home-dashboard-field`, draws every supported light, climate and humidifier entity, room sensor, the weather, every cover and alarm panel, every Owlet sock and every mode (input_boolean or switch) by area, and a sound machine's sound where its YAML picks `card: sound` ([deploy.md](deploy.md#the-field-dashboard)); it hasn't been loaded in a live Home Assistant yet.

## Design rules

The every-skin hard rules in AGENTS.md apply. `src/skins/field/styles/rules.test.ts` checks the ones below that CSS can show.

- **Three parts, one meaning each.**
  - **Key** (flat-topped, just raised): press it (`usePress`, shared with orb). A key that can't be pressed lies flat, in dim ink; the chosen key of a group (a mode, a preset) stays down, pressed in, in full ink, so the current choice never reads as out of reach. Its glyph is the domain's (a bulb for a light), so the key is also the row's identity.
  - **Fader** (a printed legend with a rule out to its end, a thin dark slot, a dotted scale, a round thumb): slide or set along it. A tap jumps the thumb; a drag follows the finger (`useSlider`, shared with orb).
  - **Screen** (always dark, in both finishes): read it, never press it. A pictogram and a big thin figure on the left, up to two meta lines on the right.
- **A reading has nothing to press.** Where a control has its key, a reading has its **glyph**: the domain's glyph on a plate that lies flat, as a key that can't be pressed does, so the row keeps its identity in the same place (`Glyph`; not a button, no LED). Where a control has its fader, a reading has **today**: a printed legend with the day's low and high over a strip of screen, one column per hour, in the fader's 44 px (`Today`). Only the name opens its details.
- **A lit LED says on.** The LED in a key's corner is the one sign that something is on (`--fd-led-lit`); the screen agrees, with a lit pictogram and a bright figure, or `OFF` in the dim screen ink.
- **Colour names a parameter**, the way a knob's colour names what it sets on TE's devices. `--fd-param-a`, orange, is a module's first parameter (a light's brightness; climate's single target or lower heat threshold). `--fd-param-b`, blue, is its second (a light's warmth; climate's upper cool threshold). The parameter colour appears on its fader thumb and screen pictogram, not as a heat/cool signal; words and mode geometry carry that meaning. A reading sets nothing, so it has no parameter colour: its pictogram lights in the figure's ink, and its meter and an activity's (climate's runtime) are neutral.
- **The screen speaks in fixed places**, like a segment display. Numbers keep their digit count, and the leading digits are unlit segments (`080%`), drawn by CSS so screen readers hear "80%". A pictogram is always there unlit, and lights up to its level from the bottom; a light's bulb keeps its base lit while on, so 3% still reads lit. A missing reading appears as width-specific blank segments (`---` or `----`), never a plausible number.
- **Details open in a panel**, never by growing the module: tap the name (it carries a small arrow) or press and hold the key. The panel is the module's plate grown into a dialog: a centred panel on wider screens, a bottom sheet on phones that its header drags down to close (`useSheet`, shared with orb). Its screen is taller and carries a second channel on its right. Under the primary controls come capability-specific groups under printed legends, today as a 24-hour meter on a strip of screen, then facts in small print.
- **A warning is a marker and words**, never a colour: an outlined warning sign before its words, said as "Warning: …" (`Warn`). A warning about the device rather than its reading, such as a low battery, is printed on the plate in place of the area, where it has the module's full width in either footprint (`Module`'s `warning`); the screen keeps to the reading. Something about the device that is a choice, not a fault, such as a baby monitor's base station switched off, takes the same line in plain words, without the marker (`Module`'s `note`), and gives way to a warning. Field has no warning colour and no glow to raise.
- **Unavailable** blanks the screen, says `UNAVAILABLE` and when it was last seen on the meta line, and lays every shown control flat; a key keeps its place but drops its state word, since the state isn't known, and stays focusable (`aria-disabled`, `usePress`'s `unavailable`, as orb's orb does), so a hold still opens its details. A plus or minus step at its bound lies flat the same way, so focus never drops off it. It never fabricates a reading or target.
- **Type** is Archivo, a geometric sans, set wide (`--fd-wide`), upper-case for printed labels and names, and light for the screen's figure. Names are upper-cased by CSS, so the accessible name keeps its case. Nothing printed is smaller than 10 px, so a wall panel's labels read from arm's length: legends, facts and the words under keys at 10 px, the area and the screen's meta lines at 10.5 px. Storybook loads Archivo in `.storybook/preview-head.html`; without it the stack falls back to the system sans.
- **Keys** latch or act once. A latching key (power, a colour) has an LED; a key that acts once (a quick level) has none, so a lit LED only ever means on: the lamp, or the colour it is showing.
- **A key's word is what pressing it does** wherever that isn't the plain inverse of on and off. A plain on/off key (a light, a mode, a sound) may print `On` or `Off`, since its LED makes the latch plain and it is announced as pressed. Any other key prints its verb (`Turn on`, `Turn off`, `Close`, `Open`, `Stop`) and says it in its label, so it isn't also announced as pressed; its LED still says what is on or open, and the screen says the state or mode.
- **A lamp's own colour is data, not a signal.** The `--fd-lamp-*` tokens appear only as swatches: a square on each colour key, and the lens pictogram on the screen while the lamp shows that colour. The names and the hue each covers are shared with orb (`src/domains/light/color.ts`); each skin draws its own swatches.
- **Footprints:** `standard` is a 160 px module (header, screen, a 44 px control row); `compact` is a 64 px row (key, name and area, a fixed-width screen). The height never changes with state or entity type, nor with whether the module has an area. Container queries simplify the inside at narrow widths, the same for every card (`module.css`): under 240 px a module keeps its figure and one meta line and its pictogram goes, since the key or glyph below names the device; under 280 px a row's screen narrows and its pictogram goes.
- **Scoped tokens.** Every custom property starts with `--fd-`, including the easings (`--fd-ease-out`, `--fd-ease-in-out`), so field and orb can share a page. No colour outside `styles/tokens.css`. The page is a plain body, with no pattern.
- **Motion:** presses take `--fd-press` (120 ms) on `transform` and `box-shadow`; a fader jump moves its rail with `transform`; the pictogram fills with `clip-path`; the LED fades its colour; an Owlet sock's heart flashes its colour while readings arrive. Reduced motion keeps colour and fill changes and drops the movement, and turns a flash into a slow swell. Hover only under `(hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)`.

## From the exploration and the references

Kept selectively from the exploration (`ha_01_universal_home_assistant_field_console.html`): the alloy and black finishes, module plate, keys with LEDs, dark screen and orange parameter. Climate also keeps the useful current-temperature-first readout and compact mode key bank; its normalized entity capabilities, not the prototype's fixed console, decide what appears.

Changed after looking at TE's own product photos (OP–1 field, OP–XY, TX–6, EP–133 K.O. II): monospace became a wide geometric sans; orange moved from "on" to naming a parameter; keys and plates lost most of their depth; the striped fader thumb became a round one in a thin slot; the screen gained a pictogram; the fader gained a printed legend; the perforated page became plain.

Left out: screws, scanlines, speaker grilles, entity ids as labels, blur crossfades and staggered entry, click sounds, and the console chrome (banks, patch bay, command bus).

## Light

- **Module:** the power key (bulb glyph, LED), the screen (a bulb pictogram filled with the brightness, its base lit whenever the lamp is on so 3% still reads lit; the figure; its warmth, or its colour's name; since when), and the brightness fader. On/off lamps: one wide key with its state word, no fader. Compact: key, name and area, screen.
- **Panel:** the screen with a second channel: the lamp's colour while it shows one (a lens in that colour and its name, "Amber"), otherwise its warmth (sun pictogram, kelvin, its name: "Warm white"). Then the power key and brightness fader; quick levels 5, 25, 50, 100 (Night, Dim, Half, Full); the warmth fader in blue, flat while the lamp is off; a colour lamp's seven colour keys (Red, Amber, Yellow, Green, Blue, Purple, White), the one it shows latched, and choosing one turns it on, four and three on a phone so each name fits; today; last changed (or last seen) and what the lamp can do. A lamp with both shows a warmth or a colour, never both, as Home Assistant reports it.
- **Unavailable:** blank segments, "Unavailable" and when it was last seen, on the panel's screen too; the key spans the row with no state word and the brightness fader goes, since the brightness isn't known; the warmth fader draws no thumb, and the quick levels and colour keys lie flat.

## Climate

- **Module:** the thermostat key switches the entity and its LED is the on signal. The screen leads with room temperature, with target and HVAC action secondary. Heat, cool and the other modes use distinct geometry and words, never colour. Heat, cool and auto put their target on one orange fader; under 240 px, where the screen keeps one meta line, it keeps what the unit is doing, since the fader's legend prints the target. `heat_cool` uses an ordered two-thumb fader: orange is “heat below”, blue is “cool above”, and its native sliders never cross. Off becomes one wide **Turn on** key; dry and fan-only have no temperature target, so their key spans the row as **Turn off**, the mode on the screen, its pictogram lit in the figure's ink since no parameter is set. The key is a round thermostat's dial, so a column tells it from a room sensor's thermometer. Compact is the same 64 px key/name/screen row as light.
- **Panel:** the second screen channel holds the target while on, or the last room reading while off. It repeats the primary control, adds exact plus/minus steps for a single target (**Fine target**, one step of the entity's at a time) or a heat/cool range (**Fine range**), and draws only the entity's supported HVAC modes, presets and fan modes as keys. The current choice is printed in the legend and its key stays down, pressed in; it stays focusable and is announced as pressed (`Key`'s `chosen`), and pressing it again does nothing. No choice key gets a second “on” LED. Today uses the neutral screen ink because runtime is status, not a parameter. Humidity, last change or last seen, and the supported temperature range finish the panel.
- **Unavailable:** blank segments and “Unavailable”, one flat key across the row with no state word, no fabricated temperature or target, and a neutral unavailable history meter when history exists.

## Sensor

- **Module:** a reading. The thermometer glyph on its flat plate, then today's temperature meter with its low and high, where light and climate have their key and fader. The screen leads with the temperature, its thermometer filling from the bulb (lit to the bulb at −10 °C, to the top at 40 °C) in the figure's ink. The two meta lines take, most important first: the humidity (`44% RH`), comfort in words ("Cool, dry"; none outdoors), and whether it is rising or falling. A low battery (15% or less) takes the area's line under the name, behind the warning marker. A sensor with only humidity makes humidity the figure, behind a drop. Under 240 px the screen keeps the figure and one meta line, and the pictogram goes, since the glyph below names the reading. Compact: glyph, name and area (or the low battery), screen.
- **Panel:** humidity on the screen's second channel (a drop filled to the humidity), or the module's meta lines for a sensor without it. Then today's temperature and today's humidity, each with its low and high; then comfort and trend in words, or the battery warning with "replace soon"; the battery level and when it last reported.
- **Unavailable:** blank segments, "Unavailable" and when it was last seen, and the hours it was gone dashed on the meters; today's low and high stay, since they were read. A sensor that has never reported says "No reading yet" over blank segments.

## Weather

- **Module:** a reading. The sun-and-cloud glyph on its flat plate, then today's temperature meter, where light and climate have their key and fader; its legend carries today's low and high in whole degrees, from the readings so far, the rest of today's hourly forecast and the daily forecast (`2°–8°`; `-3° to -1°` below zero, so a dash never meets a minus). The screen leads with the temperature under the sky's pictogram (sun, moon by night, cloud, fog, rain, snow, storm, wind), lit in the figure's ink. The two meta lines take, most urgent first: a warning, behind the marker (poor air over US AQI 100, gusts from 50 km/h, UV from very high); else rain or snow in the next 12 hours (`70% rain at 14:00`), or when falling rain stops (`Rain until 16:00`); else the condition. Then how it feels (`Feels 4°`). More than one warning shows the most urgent with a count of the rest (`Hazardous air +2`), or under 260 px the count alone (`3 warnings`), and is spoken in full. These warnings are about the reading, so they stay on the screen. Under 240 px the screen keeps the figure and one meta line, and the pictogram goes. Compact: glyph, place, screen; a row has no meta lines, so the first one takes the area's line under the name, a warning behind its marker (weather has no area of its own).
- **Panel:** how it feels on the screen's second channel. Then every warning by name and the sky in words (`Partly cloudy · 70% rain at 14:00`); today's temperatures with their low and high; the next eight hours and the next five days on strips of screen (the hour or day, the sky's pictogram, the temperature or high over low, a chance of rain from 20%), each spoken as one description; then every reading the entity has, printed on the plate under **Now** (dew point, humidity, wind with its direction and gusts, air quality, UV, pressure), a warning's reading behind the marker; and when it last updated. Without a forecast yet, **Next hours** says so.
- **Unavailable:** blank segments, an unlit cloud, "Unavailable" and when it was last seen, and the hours it was gone dashed on the meter; today's low and high from what was read stay. The panel draws no forecast, sky or readings. A weather that has no temperature yet says "No temperature yet" over blank segments.

## Toggle

- **Module:** a mode you switch on and off (Do Not Disturb, Quiet, Guest), one `input_boolean` or `switch`. One key across the row, carrying the mode's own glyph (a bell struck through, a moon, guests, a suitcase, waves; power when it has none) and its state word, the way an on/off lamp's key does; its LED is the on signal. The screen says `ON` or `OFF` beside the mode's pictogram, lit in the figure's ink while on: a mode sets no parameter, so nothing on it wears a parameter's colour, and the entity's own colour (orb's `color`) isn't drawn. The meta lines say until when it stays on, then since when (`Until 12:54`, `Since 11:02`), or since when alone. Compact: the square key, name and area, screen; the key's glyph tells a column of modes apart.
- **Panel:** when it turns itself off on the screen's second channel, behind a clock (`12:54`, "Turns off"), while it is on for a while; otherwise the module's meta lines. Then the key; what the mode does, in a sentence; and, given `onTurnOnFor`, a key per way to turn it on (30 min, 1 hour, Morning, Until off), what each ends at printed under it, under **Turn on for**, or **Keep on** while it is on, with the one it was turned on with chosen. Home Assistant has no timed-on service for either domain, so the host owns the timer, as on orb's tile. Four keys in a row, two by two on a phone. Today, neutral, with how long it has been on; when it last changed, and whether it is a switch or a toggle helper.
- **Unavailable:** blank segments, "Unavailable" and when it was last seen; the key lies flat with no state word, the panel's keys lie flat, and the hours it was gone are dashed on the meter.

## Humidifier

- **Module:** one `humidifier.*` entity, a humidifier or, by its `device_class`, a dehumidifier. The key switches it and its LED is the on signal; its glyph is mist rising from the unit, or a dehumidifier's grille over its tank, so a column of them tells the two apart. The screen leads with the room's humidity (`038%`) beside a drop, filled to the target and lit in the target's colour (orange, the module's one parameter) while on; a unit with no target lights it in the figure's ink. The meta lines say what the target does, then what the unit is doing: `Humidify to 45%` or `Dry to 50%`, then Humidifying, Drying or Idle (the mode when it reports no action). While on with a target, the target fader takes the rest of the row, in whole percent between the entity's bounds. Off, the screen says `OFF` with the room's humidity on the meta line, and the key spans the row as **Turn on**, as climate's does: Home Assistant keeps the setpoint while off but setting it doesn't turn the unit on, so the fader waits. A unit that has no target spans the key as **Turn off**, the mode on the screen's meta line, and its screen says `ON` when it reports no humidity, never a made-up number. Under 240 px the screen keeps the figure and the one meta line the module doesn't already say (what the unit is doing beside the fader's legend, the room's humidity beside `OFF`), and the drop goes, since the key names the unit. Compact: key, name and area, screen.
- **Panel:** the target on the screen's second channel (a drop and `045%`, "Humidify to" or "Dry to") while on, the room's humidity while off, or the mode for a unit with no target. Then the key and fader; exact steps of one percent under **Fine target**, lying flat at the entity's bounds; the unit's modes as keys under **Mode** when it lists more than one, the current one chosen; today, neutral, with how long it has been on; when it last changed, and whether it is a humidifier or a dehumidifier with its settable range (its kind alone while unavailable, since the range is then unknown).
- **Unavailable:** blank segments, "Unavailable" and when it was last seen; the key lies flat with no state word, the fader and mode keys go, and the hours it was gone are dashed on the meter.

## Sound

- **Module:** a sound machine's sound, one `switch` with its volume `number` beside it. One key across the row, carrying a speaker and its sound and its state word; its LED is the on signal. The screen leads with the volume (`062%`) beside a speaker filled to it, lit in the volume's colour (orange, the module's one parameter) while it plays, then `Playing` and since when. Off, the screen says `OFF`, with the volume and since when on the meta lines. A sound without a volume says `ON` or `OFF` in the figure's ink, with since when. The volume is set in the panel only, as on orb's tile: it rarely changes, and a stray tap on a fader beside a sleeping baby would jump it, so the module's row is the key alone. Under 240 px the screen keeps the figure and the first meta line, and the speaker goes, since the key names the sound. Compact: the square key, name and area, screen.
- **Panel:** the volume on the screen's second channel while off (an unlit speaker, `062%`, "Volume"), since the figure says `OFF`; playing, the figure is the volume already. Then the key, and the volume fader across the panel. Setting the volume never switches the sound, so while it is off the fader stays settable but unlit, its legend `Off · 62%`. Today, neutral, with how long it has been on; when it last changed, and whether it has a volume.
- **Unavailable:** blank segments, "Unavailable" and when it was last seen; the key lies flat with no state word, the fader goes, and the hours it was gone are dashed on the meter.

## Cover

- **Module:** one `cover.*` entity: curtains, blinds, shades, shutters, awnings, windows, garage doors, doors and gates. Position follows Home Assistant: 0 is closed, 100 is open. The key closes it while it is open and opens it while it is closed, and prints that verb (`Close`, `Open`); while it travels, it stops it (`Stop`), its glyph a stop square (a cover that can't stop keeps opening and closing). A garage door, door or gate moves only on a press and hold from the module or row, its key printing `Hold to close` or `Hold to open`: a stray tap opens its details instead, where its keys act at once. Stopping one is always a tap. Its LED is the open signal, and its glyph is the cover's kind drawn open or shut (curtains on a rail, a door under its roof, a window, a blind), so a column of covers tells them apart. The screen leads with how open it is (`070%`; closed is `000%` in the dim ink, a reading and not a blank) beside a window lit from the bottom up to it, as daylight under a blind that rolls up, in the position's colour (orange, the module's one parameter). The meta lines say `Open`, `Closed`, or while it travels `Opening` or `Closing`, then since when. The position fader takes the rest of the row, its legend `Position`, or the way it is going while it travels; its ends are magnetic, as on orb's tile (within 3% is fully closed or open). A cover with no position (most garage doors) has no fader: the key spans the row with what pressing it does, or **Stop** while it travels, and the screen says `OPEN` or `SHUT`, four places like a segment display's (`CLOSED` doesn't fit a row's screen); on its way it is neither, so the figure is blank segments, never a guessed position, and the meta line says where it is going. A garage door, door or gate left open is a warning about what it reads, so it stays on the screen behind the marker (`Open since 10:20`); a row has no meta lines, so there it takes the area's line. No glow and no warning colour, as everywhere in field. Under 240 px the screen keeps the figure and the first meta line, and the window goes, since the key names the cover. Compact: key, name and area, screen; a cover with no position lets the window go, since `OPEN` fills the row's screen.
- **Panel:** a blind's slats on the screen's second channel (blue slats and `050%`, "Slats"), otherwise the module's meta lines. Then the key and fader; **Quick positions** as keys (Closed, 25%, Half, Open), the current one chosen while it is still; for a cover with no position, **Move**: Close, Stop and Open keys that act once, the one that can't act lying flat (Stop while it is still, Open while it is open), Stop only where the cover can stop; the slats fader in blue, the module's second parameter; today, how open each hour was, in orange where the cover has a position and neutral where it only opens and closes, with what it has been since; when it last changed, and its kind with what it can do (`Blind · position, tilt`; its kind alone while unavailable).
- **Unavailable:** blank segments, "Unavailable" and when it was last seen; the key lies flat with no state word, the fader, quick positions and slats go, and the hours it was gone are dashed on the meter.

## Alarm

- **Module:** one `alarm_control_panel.*`. The screen says `ON` while armed and `OFF` (in the dim ink) while disarmed, beside a shield lit in the figure's ink while armed: arming sets no parameter, so nothing wears a parameter's colour. The meta lines say the mode (`Armed away`) or `Disarmed`, then since when. An exit or entry delay counts down in the figure's place (`0:42`), worked out from its length and when it started, as on orb's tile; without its length there's no way to know, so the figure is blank segments, never a guessed time. Arming says `Arming away` and `Leave now`; the entry delay puts what set it off behind the warning marker (`Front door opened`, else `Entry delay`) with `Disarm now`; triggered, `Triggered by Back door` behind the marker, then since when; disarmed with doors open, the door (`Back door open`), or how many and which, the count first so a long name never hides it (`2 open: Back door, Kitchen window`). No glow and no warning colour, as everywhere in field. The row is a choice key for Off and each mode the panel supports (Home, Away, Night, Vacation, Custom), the current one chosen, each with its glyph (power, a house, a door left by, a moon, a suitcase, zones) and its word where every word fits; armed in a mode the panel doesn't list (custom bypass from the panel itself), that mode is shown, never Off. Where even the glyphs can't keep 44 px (four choices under 200 px of plate, five under 252, six under 304), and through a delay, one key takes the row and prints what pressing it does: **Disarm** while armed, on its way in or triggered, **Cancel** while arming, **Arm** while disarmed, which opens the panel's mode keys (a panel with one mode arms it at once: **Arm away**); while disarming it lies flat. Its LED is the armed signal and its glyph the shield, checked while armed, marked while it needs attention. Whatever needs the code opens the panel on its keypad instead, so a tap never sends a code-guarded change; Home Assistant asks for the code to arm too unless `code_arm_required` is false. Compact: the shield key, name and line, screen; a row has no meta lines, so a warning takes the area's line behind its marker, and the mode takes it while armed or on its way (`Armed away`, `Arming away`). A view-only panel (`readOnly`, or no `onArm` and `onDisarm`) is a reading: the shield on its flat plate and today's meter where the keys would be.
- **Panel:** the screen as the module's. Then the mode keys under **Mode**, every one with its word (four in a row, three to a row from five, two on a narrow phone), and what the current mode watches in a sentence; through a delay, the one key. Doors and windows open, by name, behind the marker, with what arming would leave unguarded. Today, neutral, with how long it has been armed; when it last changed and by whom, and what the code is needed for (`Code to arm and disarm`, `Code to disarm`, `No code`, or `View only`). **Keypad:** what the code is for as its legend (`Code to arm away`), the code on a strip of screen, a dot per place, unlit to four, then the digits three by four with delete and enter (52 px keys), and Cancel. A physical keyboard types too: digits, letters for a text code, Backspace, Enter. While Home Assistant answers the screen says `Checking`; a wrong code clears for another try (`Wrong code. Try again`, behind the marker), and one that didn't go through stays, so Enter tries again. Once accepted, the panel goes back to the mode keys; a disarm that's no longer needed leaves the keypad by itself.
- **Unavailable:** blank segments, "Unavailable" and when it was last seen; the one key lies flat with no word, the mode keys go, and the hours it was gone are dashed on the meter.

## Owlet

- **Module:** an Owlet Smart Sock, its sleep state sensor with its heart rate, oxygen, charging and alert sensors found beside it. A reading: the sock on its flat plate, then today's sleep on the meter (deep sleep full, light half, awake on the baseline) with how long the baby has slept (`Asleep 9h 54m`). The screen leads with the heart rate (`121 bpm`; `072` under 100, the leading place unlit) beside a heart that beats while readings arrive, as a pulse oximeter's segment does: it rests in the dimmer screen ink and flashes to the figure's ink once every 2.4 s, a sign of life and not the measured rate (Home Assistant gets a new figure every few seconds at best, so it can't flash with the pulse). Only its colour changes, never its size or fill, since a pictogram's fill means an amount; under reduced motion it swells slowly between the two inks instead. Where the host can't tell whether readings arrive, it stays lit and still; the meta lines say the sleep state (`Deep sleep`, `Light sleep`, `Awake`, or `Sleep unknown` while the sock reads but hasn't settled) and the oxygen (`98% O₂`). The orb card leads with the sleep state; on a segment screen a word the width of `AWAKE` doesn't fit a row's screen, and a number does, so the heart rate is the figure and the sleep state is never further than the first line. Under 240 px the heart goes, as every pictogram does, but both meta lines stay, since nothing else on the module says them. **A vitals alert** (critical, low or high oxygen; low or high heart rate) is the base station sounding: about the reading, so it takes the screen's first line behind the marker (`Low oxygen`), with the oxygen beside it on the second; several show the most urgent with a count of the rest (`Critical oxygen +1`), or under 300 px the count alone (`2 alerts`), spoken in full. Field has no glow and no warning colour, so there is no pulse: the marker and its words carry it, first on the screen. **A problem with the sock or base** (sock off, disconnected, base lost power, battery critical or low) is the device's, so it takes the area's line behind the marker, as does a reading that stopped: when no reading has changed for 3 minutes the heart stops and goes out, the figure dims, and the line says `No reading since 11:49` (a row: `Stopped 11:49`, keeping the time when the update has a day).
