# Field skin

A second skin, `src/skins/field/`, drawn after Teenage Engineering's EP–133 K.O. II: a flat light-grey body, printed labels, flat-topped keys, a thin fader with a round thumb, and a dark segment-style screen. It sits on the same entity models and Home Assistant hooks as orb (`useHaLight` and the rest); only the drawing differs. Stories are under **Field** in Storybook, in the same groups as orb's except Bundles, which are orb's alone, with a **Finish** toolbar (Auto, Alloy, Black). Foundations → Design rules draws this file with live stories beside its sections (edit it here, not there), Foundations → Palette shows the parts below and what each means, and Dashboards → Lovelace card is its whole dashboard from YAML.

Status: light, climate and sensor are implemented, each with a detail panel; sensor is the first reading, the pattern weather and the other read-only cards follow. The Lovelace card, `custom:home-dashboard-field`, draws every supported light and climate entity and room sensor by area ([deploy.md](deploy.md#the-field-dashboard)); it hasn't been loaded in a live Home Assistant yet.

## Design rules

The every-skin hard rules in AGENTS.md apply. `src/skins/field/styles/rules.test.ts` checks the ones below that CSS can show.

- **Three parts, one meaning each.**
  - **Key** (flat-topped, just raised): press it (`usePress`, shared with orb). A key that can't be pressed lies flat. Its glyph is the domain's (a bulb for a light), so the key is also the row's identity.
  - **Fader** (a printed legend with a rule out to its end, a thin dark slot, a dotted scale, a round thumb): slide or set along it. A tap jumps the thumb; a drag follows the finger (`useSlider`, shared with orb).
  - **Screen** (always dark, in both finishes): read it, never press it. A pictogram and a big thin figure on the left, up to two meta lines on the right.
- **A reading has nothing to press.** Where a control has its key, a reading has its **glyph**: the domain's glyph on a plate that lies flat, as a key that can't be pressed does, so the row keeps its identity in the same place (`Glyph`; not a button, no LED). Where a control has its fader, a reading has **today**: a printed legend with the day's low and high over a strip of screen, one column per hour, in the fader's 44 px (`Today`). Only the name opens its details.
- **A lit LED says on.** The LED in a key's corner is the one sign that something is on (`--fd-led-lit`); the screen agrees, with a lit pictogram and a bright figure, or `OFF` in the dim screen ink.
- **Colour names a parameter**, the way a knob's colour names what it sets on TE's devices. `--fd-param-a`, orange, is a module's first parameter (a light's brightness; climate's single target or lower heat threshold). `--fd-param-b`, blue, is its second (a light's warmth; climate's upper cool threshold). The parameter colour appears on its fader thumb and screen pictogram, not as a heat/cool signal; words and mode geometry carry that meaning. A reading sets nothing, so it has no parameter colour: its pictogram lights in the figure's ink, and its meter and an activity's (climate's runtime) are neutral.
- **The screen speaks in fixed places**, like a segment display. Numbers keep their digit count, and the leading digits are unlit segments (`080%`), drawn by CSS so screen readers hear "80%". A pictogram is always there unlit, and lights up to its level from the bottom; a light's bulb keeps its base lit while on, so 3% still reads lit. A missing reading appears as width-specific blank segments (`---` or `----`), never a plausible number.
- **Details open in a panel**, never by growing the module: tap the name (it carries a small arrow) or press and hold the key. The panel is the module's plate grown into a dialog: a centred panel on wider screens, a bottom sheet on phones that its header drags down to close (`useSheet`, shared with orb). Its screen is taller and carries a second channel on its right. Under the primary controls come capability-specific groups under printed legends, today as a 24-hour meter on a strip of screen, then facts in small print.
- **A warning is a marker and words**, never a colour: an outlined warning sign before its words, said as "Warning: …" (`Warn`). A warning about the device rather than its reading, such as a low battery, is printed on the plate in place of the area, where it has the module's full width in either footprint (`Module`'s `warning`); the screen keeps to the reading. Field has no warning colour and no glow to raise.
- **Unavailable** blanks the screen, says `UNAVAILABLE` and when it was last seen on the meta line, and lays every shown control flat and disabled. It never fabricates a reading or target.
- **Type** is Archivo, a geometric sans, set wide (`--fd-wide`), upper-case for printed labels and names, and light for the screen's figure. Names are upper-cased by CSS, so the accessible name keeps its case. Storybook loads Archivo in `.storybook/preview-head.html`; without it the stack falls back to the system sans.
- **Keys** latch or act once. A latching key (power, a colour) has an LED; a key that acts once (a quick level) has none, so a lit LED only ever means on: the lamp, or the colour it is showing.
- **A lamp's own colour is data, not a signal.** The `--fd-lamp-*` tokens appear only as swatches: a square on each colour key, and the lens pictogram on the screen while the lamp shows that colour. The names and the hue each covers are shared with orb (`src/domains/light/color.ts`); each skin draws its own swatches.
- **Footprints:** `standard` is a 160 px module (header, screen, a 44 px control row); `compact` is a 64 px row (key, name and area, a fixed-width screen). The height never changes with state or entity type. Container queries simplify the inside at narrow widths.
- **Scoped tokens.** Every custom property starts with `--fd-`, including the easings (`--fd-ease-out`, `--fd-ease-in-out`), so field and orb can share a page. No colour outside `styles/tokens.css`. The page is a plain body, with no pattern.
- **Motion:** presses take `--fd-press` (120 ms) on `transform` and `box-shadow`; a fader jump moves its rail with `transform`; the pictogram fills with `clip-path`; the LED fades its colour. Reduced motion keeps colour and fill changes and drops the movement. Hover only under `(hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)`.

## From the exploration and the references

Kept selectively from the exploration (`ha_01_universal_home_assistant_field_console.html`): the alloy and black finishes, module plate, keys with LEDs, dark screen and orange parameter. Climate also keeps the useful current-temperature-first readout and compact mode key bank; its normalized entity capabilities, not the prototype's fixed console, decide what appears.

Changed after looking at TE's own product photos (OP–1 field, OP–XY, TX–6, EP–133 K.O. II): monospace became a wide geometric sans; orange moved from "on" to naming a parameter; keys and plates lost most of their depth; the striped fader thumb became a round one in a thin slot; the screen gained a pictogram; the fader gained a printed legend; the perforated page became plain.

Left out: screws, scanlines, speaker grilles, entity ids as labels, blur crossfades and staggered entry, click sounds, and the console chrome (banks, patch bay, command bus).

## Light

- **Module:** the power key (bulb glyph, LED), the screen (a bulb pictogram filled with the brightness, its base lit whenever the lamp is on so 3% still reads lit; the figure; its warmth, or its colour's name; since when), and the brightness fader. On/off lamps: one wide key with its state word, no fader. Compact: key, name and area, screen.
- **Panel:** the screen with a second channel: the lamp's colour while it shows one (a lens in that colour and its name, "Amber"), otherwise its warmth (sun pictogram, kelvin, its name: "Warm white"). Then the power key and brightness fader; quick levels 5, 25, 50, 100 (Night, Dim, Half, Full); the warmth fader in blue, flat while the lamp is off; a colour lamp's seven colour keys (Red, Amber, Yellow, Green, Blue, Purple, White), the one it shows latched, and choosing one turns it on; today; last changed (or last seen) and what the lamp can do. A lamp with both shows a warmth or a colour, never both, as Home Assistant reports it.

## Climate

- **Module:** the thermostat key switches the entity and its LED is the on signal. The screen leads with room temperature, with target and HVAC action secondary. Heat, cool and the other modes use distinct geometry and words, never colour. Heat, cool and auto put their target on one orange fader. `heat_cool` uses an ordered two-thumb fader: orange is “heat below”, blue is “cool above”, and its native sliders never cross. Off becomes one wide **Turn on** key; dry and fan-only keep one wide mode key because they have no temperature target. Compact is the same 64 px key/name/screen row as light.
- **Panel:** the second screen channel holds the target while on, or the last room reading while off. It repeats the primary control, adds exact plus/minus steps for a heat/cool range, and draws only the entity's supported HVAC modes, presets and fan modes as keys. The current choice is printed in the legend and its key lies flat; it stays focusable and is announced as pressed (`Key`'s `chosen`), and pressing it again does nothing. No choice key gets a second “on” LED. Today uses the neutral screen ink because runtime is status, not a parameter. Humidity, last change or last seen, and the supported temperature range finish the panel.
- **Unavailable:** blank segments and “Unavailable”, one flat disabled key, no fabricated temperature or target, and a neutral unavailable history meter when history exists.

## Sensor

- **Module:** a reading. The thermometer glyph on its flat plate, then today's temperature meter with its low and high, where light and climate have their key and fader. The screen leads with the temperature, its thermometer filling from the bulb (lit to the bulb at −10 °C, to the top at 40 °C) in the figure's ink. The two meta lines take, most important first: the humidity (`44% RH`), comfort in words ("Cool, dry"; none outdoors), and whether it is rising or falling. A low battery (15% or less) takes the area's line under the name, behind the warning marker. A sensor with only humidity makes humidity the figure, behind a drop. Under 240 px the screen keeps the figure and one meta line, and the pictogram goes, since the glyph below names the reading. Compact: glyph, name and area (or the low battery), screen.
- **Panel:** humidity on the screen's second channel (a drop filled to the humidity), or the module's meta lines for a sensor without it. Then today's temperature and today's humidity, each with its low and high; then comfort and trend in words, or the battery warning with "replace soon"; the battery level and when it last reported.
- **Unavailable:** blank segments, "Unavailable" and when it was last seen, and the hours it was gone dashed on the meters; today's low and high stay, since they were read. A sensor that has never reported says "No reading yet" over blank segments.
