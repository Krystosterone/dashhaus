# AGENTS.md

React + TypeScript skins for a custom Home Assistant dashboard, developed and shown in Storybook. This is a component library, not a runnable app; the checks are the ones under Commands. The current skins are Orb (`src/skins/orb/`, `@dashhaus/orb`) and Field (`src/skins/field/`, `@dashhaus/field`), both fixed-version workspace packages; the entity models and Home Assistant logic under them are skin-free. `@dashhaus/bundle` ships the prebuilt one-file Lovelace bundle and `packages/create-dashhaus-skin` scaffolds a standalone skin outside this repository (`spec/build-a-skin.md`). The spec lives in `spec/`, written for agents and maintainers and read by tests, generators and Storybook; `docs/` is the documentation site for people, published to GitHub Pages (dated research reports stay in `docs/research/`, outside the site). The spec is split: `spec/orb.md` contains Orb's design and composition rules, `spec/field.md` contains Field's, and `spec/cards.md` contains per-card behaviour. Consult the affected skin's rules and the card behaviour before changing it. `spec/contract.md` is what a third-party skin may rely on from `@dashhaus/dashboard`. `spec/performance.md` documents the opt-in marks and the live production measurement command. Open research — an unimplemented decision or an unrun validation — lives in `research/README.md`, with the component gaps the implemented contracts still imply in `research/mechanism-backlog.md`; an implemented decision lives in the doc, skill or code that owns it, not in a note.

## Vocabulary

One word per thing, the same in code, Storybook and the docs.

- **Card** — one model drawn on a dashboard, Home Assistant's word for it. Every skin keeps its cards in `src/skins/<skin>/cards/<name>/` and files their stories under `<Skin>/Cards/<Name>`. "Widget" is the old name; don't bring it back.
- **Model** — the normalized object a card draws, from `useHa<Name>`. A **direct model** is one Home Assistant entity (a light, a thermostat, a cover); a **composed model** is a primary entity plus companions the hook composes (found beside it, or given as options): a sensor with its device's humidity and battery, a sound machine's switch and volume, the weather with air quality and sun, an Owlet sock's sensors and base station. Composition lives in the entity type and the hook, never in the card: every card still takes one `entity` prop, so a dashboard, layout or YAML treats every card alike. "Device card" describes a composed model from one device; it is not a third card type.
- **Interaction** — what a card lets you do: a **control** has a primary action, a **reading** only shows values. It is a trait of the props (an alarm can be view-only, a reading can carry a small action), never a third card type.
- **Tile** — orb's card ready for a dashboard: the card in its size container with its details sheet wired in (`<Name>Tile`, from `detailsTile()`). `<Name>Card` draws the card itself, and draws it again as `variant="sheet"` inside the sheet.
- **Module** — field's card: the plate with its key, screen and fader (a reading's glyph, screen and today), carrying its own detail panel (`<Name>Module`). It is to field what the tile is to orb; field's YAML lists them under `modules:`.
- **Details** — what a card opens from its name or a press and hold: orb's sheet, field's panel. Stories say Details for both.
- **Footprint** — a card's fixed height: `compact`, `standard`, `featured` in orb; `compact`, `standard` in field.
- **Card code** — the two letters that prefix a card's CSS classes (`lw-`), and the `code` `useCard` takes.
- **Bundle** — orb only: several cards drawn as one outer card (joined, lead, titled).
- **Dashboard** — a page of cards and what frames them: `DashboardPage`, `Section`, `CardGrid`, `Columns`, `Cell`, the `Header` and the `NavBar`. The header and the nav bar are page furniture, not cards.
- **Foundation** — a story about a skin's rules rather than one card: its palette, colour temperature, compact rows, worst-case data side by side.
- **Connected** — a card bound to the store: `Ha<Name>Tile` in orb, `Ha<Name>Module` in field, on the shared `useHa<Name>` hook. Its stories are under `<Skin>/Connected/<Name>`.
- **Lovelace card** — the custom element Home Assistant loads (`custom:dashhaus-orb`, `custom:dashhaus-field`); it draws a whole dashboard from its YAML. Always say Lovelace card for it, so "card" alone keeps meaning one model's card. Inside its YAML, `cards:` lists cards and `card:` picks which card draws an entity.
- **Primitive** — a headless interaction hook in `packages/core/src/primitives/`: behaviour, no markup.
- **Part** — a skin's styled control in its `shared/`, drawn on a primitive where it has behaviour: orb's `Orb`, `TogglePill`, `Chip`, `Stepper`, `Track`; field's `Key`, `Fader`, `RangeFader`, `Screen`, `Meter`. A card is built from parts and brings only what is its own (a track's fill, a screen's figure). Each skin's **Foundations → Parts** story shows its parts across their states.
- **Seed** — demo data for one example entity, in a card's `demo/`; `<NAME>_WORST_CASE` are its worst-case seeds.

## Commands

- `npm install`
- `npm run storybook` — dev server at http://localhost:6006
- `npm run build-storybook` — static build in `storybook-static/`
- `npm run build:ha` — the Lovelace card as one file, `dist/dashhaus.js`, copied to `packages/bundle/dist/dashhaus.js` so the repo deploys the same artifact a bundle consumer installs
- `npm run build:packages` — build `packages/{core,dashboard}/dist` and `src/skins/{orb,field}/dist` (ESM JavaScript, `.d.ts`, copied fixtures and stylesheets) plus the scaffolder's `dist` for a packed install; the workspace keeps reading source through the `source` export condition
- `npm run docs` — the documentation site's dev server (Astro Starlight, `docs/`); `npm run build:docs` builds it to `docs/dist/` with the static Storybook under `docs/dist/storybook/`, as GitHub Pages serves it
- `npm run release:version -- <x.y.z>` — the fixed version across every public package and their internal `@dashhaus` ranges; run `npm install --package-lock-only` after it
- `npm run release:pack` — build and pack the seven public tarballs (`dashhaus-{core,dashboard,deploy,orb,field,bundle}-*.tgz`, `create-dashhaus-skin-*.tgz`) with release manifests (no `private`, no `source`, pinned ranges); the bundle and scaffolder are built by the same command
- `npm run verify:packages` — install the tarballs into a clean app and check imports, typecheck, the deploy bin and tarball contents; `npm run release:check` runs pack then verify (see `spec/release.md`)
- `npm run deploy [-- …]` — the `@dashhaus/deploy` CLI (`node packages/deploy/bin.js --help`): build the config's bundle, copy it into Home Assistant, register the resource, create the dashboards `dashboard.config.mjs` declares the first time; `--config <file>` points at another repo's config and `--skip-build` uses its prebuilt bundle (settings in `.env` beside the config, see `spec/deploy.md`)
- `npm run perf:collect` — measure the deployed Orb and Field dashboards in Chromium against the live Home Assistant: a cold load, a details opening and one reversible control action, with the bundle's opt-in marks and browser timings in a JSON report (`perf/reports/`); it restores the entity and reads `.env` beside the config (see `spec/performance.md`)
- `npm run typecheck` — `tsc --noEmit` for the root `tsconfig.json`, each public package's `tsconfig.json` and the docs' samples (`docs/examples/tsconfig.json`), incremental, with the state in `node_modules/.cache/tsc`
- `npm run lint` — `eslint .` (flat config in `eslint.config.js`), cached in `node_modules/.cache/eslint`
- `npm run test` — `vitest run --config config/vitest.config.ts`; one project per public package plus the root app, each with its own include and environment. Unit tests live next to their module as `*.test.{ts,tsx}` in `src/`, `packages/*/src/` and `scripts/`, and the repo's cross-skin checks live in `verify/`
- `npm run test-storybook` — every story must render (smoke) and pass axe (a11y) in Chromium; failures print the rule, impact, and target elements. Like `perf:collect`, the dx journeys' screenshots and details check, and the docs screenshots, it launches the pinned Playwright's Chromium unless `DASHHAUS_CHROMIUM` names an existing Chromium or Chrome executable (`scripts/chromium.js`; a missing path fails before a browser starts)
- `npm run test:unit` — every suite except the generator temp-copy runs (`vitest run --config config/vitest.config.ts --exclude "scripts/**"`); CI's unit shards
- `npm run test:generators` — the generator suites, `scripts/new-card/index.test.ts` and `scripts/new-skin/index.test.ts` (`vitest run --config config/vitest.config.ts scripts`), over dry runs and the `new-card`/`new-skin` temp-copy runs; CI's `generators` lane
- `npm run check` — typecheck + lint + test, the one-shot gate. Run it before every change is finished.
- `npm run new-card -- <name> --code <xx> --domain <ha_domain> [--kind control|reading]` — scaffold and register a new card (see Adding a card)
- `npm run new-card -- <name> --skin field [--kind control|reading]` — draw a domain orb already has in field (see Adding an existing domain to another skin)
- `npm run new-skin -- <lower-case-kebab-name> [--study] [--dry-run]` — create and register a neutral, working skin shell (see Adding a skin and `spec/skins.md`); `--study` makes a study, the same shell marked, filed under Storybook's Studies and never shipped (spec/skins.md → Studies)

The toolchain is declared once, and CI reads the same declarations: `.nvmrc` selects Node 22 (root `engines` holds the `>=22.9.0` floor the pinned npm needs) and `packageManager` pins npm 11.18, which CI enables with Corepack. Another npm rewrites `package-lock.json` (npm 10 drops `libc` and flips `devOptional`; npm 11.6 does too), so a wrong npm fails instead: root `devEngines.packageManager` stops npm 10.9 and later on every command before it reads or writes anything (`EBADDEVENGINES`), and an older npm, which ignores that field, fails the root `preinstall` (`scripts/check-npm.js`) with the fix. The fix is `corepack enable npm`, then `git checkout package-lock.json` if an install already ran. There is no `engine-strict`: it would also hold every dependency's `engines` to the running Node. `verify/toolchain.test.ts` keeps the floor equal to the pin. Local Node 26 still runs every command.

Node-run ESM uses `.js` under `"type": "module"`; declarations use `.d.ts`. A file named `dashboard.config.mjs` is the public deploy config wherever it lives (the repo root, the scaffolder's template, the docs sample), and extension checks match that filename rather than a fixed path list. ESLint rejects any other first-party `.mjs` or `.d.mts`.

## Verifying changes

1. `npm run check` — typecheck, lint, and unit tests.
2. `npm run test-storybook` — every story renders and passes axe; failures name the rule, impact, and elements. Where `npx playwright install chromium` can't fetch the pinned build, set `DASHHAUS_CHROMIUM=/path/to/chromium`.
3. `npm run storybook` — open the affected card's All states, Sizes, Footprints, Compact, Worst case and Details stories, and check every width and footprint. For Orb-wide changes, also inspect Orb → Foundations → Worst case.
4. `npm run build:docs` — when `docs/` or the scaffolder's templates change; builds the site and its Storybook copy as the Docs workflow serves them.
5. `npm run release:check` — when a package manifest, bin, export or module filename changes; packs the seven tarballs and installs them into a clean app.

CI runs steps 1 and 2 on every push and pull request: the `unit_checks` lane, two unit-test shards, the two generator jobs and the `dx` clean-room journeys in parallel back the required `check` job, and two Storybook shards back the required `storybook` job (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`). The Docs workflow runs step 4 on every push and pull request; `unit_checks` runs step 5's pack and verify.

Unit tests cover pure logic, the interaction primitives and shared hooks (kelvin, track position, formatting, alarm mapping and modes, nav resolution, `usePress`, `useSlider`, `useRangeSlider`, `useSheet`, `useKeypad`, `useNow`, `useDelayLeft`, `useScrollSpy`, `useHashPage`), and the deploy loader, CLI and dashboard reconciliation (`packages/deploy/src/*.test.ts`); stories are the documentation and the visual check. New pure logic gets a `*.test.ts` beside it. `src/skins/orb/styles/rules.test.ts` encodes the CSS design rules — colour tokens, the UI language (no hatching or soft accent), compositor-only motion, `--ease-*` easing, hover under the capability media query, track `touch-action` — so changing one of those rules means updating the test. Do not add another test runner or linter.

Checks that execute a package's binary resolve it from that package's `package.json` (`bin`, `exports`); never hard-code a module filename, so a rename cannot leave a test pointing at a missing entry.

## Layout

The code is split by responsibility, so a whole different UI, a skin, can sit on the same logic. `packages/core` is a workspace package, `@dashhaus/core`, holding the headless foundation; `packages/dashboard` is `@dashhaus/dashboard`, the Lovelace framework every skin's card draws on; `packages/deploy` is `@dashhaus/deploy`, the Node CLI that reads a dashboard repo's config, builds its bundle, uploads it and reconciles its dashboards. These three, the two reference skins, `packages/bundle` and `packages/create-dashhaus-skin` are the public packages: their manifests keep `private: true` in the repo and declare a workspace-only `source` condition plus built `dist` export targets (the deploy CLI ships its runnable sources); `npm run release:pack` strips `private` and `source` into publishable tarballs (`spec/release.md`). The root app (the skins, Storybook, the Lovelace bundle, `verify/` and `scripts/`) consumes the packages through their package specifiers, resolving source through the same condition. `npm install` links the workspace (`packages/*` and `src/skins/*`); importing a file inside a package from the root is a lint error. The reference skins (orb, field) are private workspace packages under `src/skins/`, named `@dashhaus/orb` and `@dashhaus/field`; a skin created by `new-skin` is a private workspace package there too, named `@dashhaus/<skin>`, and the root apps consume each through its declared exports. Each TypeScript public package owns its `tsconfig.json` (its source, tests included) and its Vitest project; its `tsconfig.build.json` extends that package config to emit `dist`. `@dashhaus/bundle` and `create-dashhaus-skin` are plain ESM with a copied `dist`. The root `tsconfig.json` (extending `config/tsconfig.base.json`) and the root Vitest project (`config/vitest.config.ts`) cover the skins and the apps; `verify/` runs in a Node `checks` project (except `tokens.test.ts`, which loads the custom-element entry and needs a DOM) and the generators in a Node project of their own.

- `packages/core/package.json` — the headless package: `private` in the repo, `sideEffects: false`, `files: ["dist", "README.md"]`, `react` a peer, `home-assistant-js-websocket` an optional peer only for `./websocket`, and `vitest`/`@testing-library/react` optional peers only for `./testing`. Its public subpaths are `.` (the Home Assistant store, provider, mappers, actions, `useHa*` hooks and headless components), `./entities` and `./entities/*` (the pure models and their logic), `./primitives`, `./hooks`, `./perf` (the opt-in performance marks the deployed bundle records), `./websocket` (the one module that imports the client), `./demo`, `./testing`, and `./fixtures/*` (the raw-state fixtures its tests reuse). Each export resolves `source` from `src/` in the workspace and `types`/`import`/`default` from `dist/` in a packed install; `npm run build:packages` emits that dist and copies the fixtures. The `.` entry has no CSS and no side effects.
- `packages/dashboard/package.json` — the Lovelace framework package: `private` in the repo, `sideEffects: false`, `files: ["dist", "README.md"]`, `react` and `react-dom` peers, `@dashhaus/core` as its one internal dependency, and `home-assistant-js-websocket` an optional peer because its entry reaches the client only through `@dashhaus/core/websocket`. Its exports follow core's source-plus-dist layout, and the source graph stays pure for `./schema` and `./layout`. Its public subpaths are `.` (the custom-element base, nav, room sensors, token scoping and rejected-command words), `./skin` (the frozen skin-author runtime: `LovelaceDashboardElement`, `registerCard`, `scopeToHost`, `autoInput`, `useActionStatus`, `LovelaceHass` and `AutoEntityEntry`), `./card` (the frame every skin's cards draw on: footprint flags, ids and aria, details state and the tile/sheet composition), `./schema` (the shared YAML reader, kind table, card reader and renderer dispatch, with the per-kind option escape hatch), `./layout` (the automatic layout's visibility, area resolution, name sort, grouping and placement); `./schema` and `./layout` are pure, so a Node config imports them. `./conformance` is the generic card preset a skin runs against its own cards: it reads a skin's `cards/` from declarations the skin supplies (its story prefix, its component and story file names, its class rule and its code) with `node:fs` and `node:path`, and imports no React, DOM, CSS or test runner, so it is Node- and test-only. `./vite` does not exist; the deploy config keeps ownership of its build. What a third-party skin may rely on from `./skin`, `./card` and `./schema`, the per-kind `options` escape hatch, and what is not stable yet is `spec/contract.md`.
- `packages/core/src/entities/` — the pure layer every skin and the Home Assistant slice sit on: one folder per entity kind with its entity and history types (`types.ts`) and pure logic (`light/kelvin.ts`, `climate/modes.ts`, `weather/conditions.ts`, `weather/readings.ts`, `toggle/durations.ts`, `owlet/readings.ts`), plus `history.ts` (`HourSample`, `Reading`), `color.ts` (`ColorName`), `format.ts` (`fmt1`), `clock.ts` (`clockFace`: a header's date and time, for every skin's header) and `severity.ts` (`Severity`: how much a card's warning may claim, advisory, urgent or alarm, each card ranking its own). `severity.ts` is consumed by alarm modes and owlet readings inside the package and by field's alert line (`src/skins/field/shared/alerts.tsx`); orb doesn't import it. `color.ts` (`ColorName`) is consumed by the toggle mapper and hook and by orb's card system (`src/skins/orb/shared/ui.tsx`); field doesn't import it. No React, no CSS, no skin imports; ESLint enforces it.
- `packages/core/src/perf.ts` — the opt-in performance marks (`@dashhaus/core/perf`): `perfEnabled`, `perfMark` and `perfMarkAfterPaint`, off unless the page has `?dashhaus_perf=1` or `localStorage["dashhaus:perf"] = "1"`. On, they record `dashhaus:*` Performance marks and `window.__dashhausPerf` for `scripts/perf/collect.js`; off, every call returns before touching a listener, the Performance API or the DOM. The store marks load connection and action lifecycle, the Lovelace element marks its connection and render, the card frame marks details, and the bundle entry marks itself. The marks and the report are documented in `spec/performance.md`.
- `packages/core/src/primitives/` — the headless interaction primitives every skin draws its controls on: behaviour and semantics, never markup or styles. Each is a hook whose result a skin spreads onto its own elements. `usePress` (`press.ts`) is a button: tap, optional press and hold (the click that ends a hold doesn't also press), optional latch (`aria-pressed`), and unavailable (`aria-disabled`, still focusable) or disabled; every latching or holding control goes through it, and a plain one-shot button stays a native `<button>`. `useSlider` and `useRangeSlider` (`slider.ts`) are a horizontal track: pointer input on a hit layer (mouse jumps on press, touch on a tap or the first sideways move, so a scroll never sets a value; `data-anim` only on a jump), and a hidden native range input for keyboard and screen readers, with its value text. A range keeps its reported ends: pointer steps count from `min`, keys step from the value, and the hidden input keeps its native `step` only while `min`, `max` and the value sit on the step's grid (otherwise `step="any"`, or the browser rounds the value it shows). `useSheet` (`sheet.ts`) is a native modal `<dialog>`: mount, show, scroll lock, `data-closing` exit, Escape and backdrop close, and drag down to close on phones. `useKeypad` (`keypad.ts`) is a code keypad: the code typed so far, digits, delete and enter from its keys or a physical keyboard, and the answer (busy, a wrong code cleared, a failed call kept for another try). A primitive used in a list goes in a small skin component (`Chip`, `Segment`), not a hand-written `aria-pressed` per item. A one-of-several group of latching keys (`ChoiceChips`, alarm segments, Field's mode keys) uses `usePress` per key, not a radiogroup primitive: roving focus and radio semantics would be new behaviour and a restyle of the chosen key. Radix was weighed and not adopted: its slider sets a value on a touch press and its dialog's focus trap fails in the card's shadow root, so each primitive is the repo's own hook. Add one only when a skin needs it. Primitives have no stories of their own (Storybook → Primitives).
- `packages/core/src/hooks/` — React hooks with no UI of their own, for every skin: `useNow`, `useDelayLeft` (an alarm's exit or entry delay, counting down), for a page's nav, `useScrollSpy` (the section being read, and a jump to one) and `useHashPage` (the page on screen, kept in the URL hash), and `useDemoClimate` (shared story state for the two climate demo folders; the one hook here that imports an entity model). The Home Assistant hooks are not here: `useHa<Name>` lives in `packages/core/src/ha/hooks/`.
- `packages/core/src/ha/` — the Home Assistant boundary (`spec/home-assistant.md`): transport, store, provider, mappers, actions and history parsing, importing entity models from `packages/core/src/entities/` and never a skin (ESLint enforces it). Any skin gets one live entity from `packages/core/src/ha/hooks/<name>.ts`, `useHa<Name>(entityId, options)` returning the mapped `entity`, today's `history` and, for a control, its `actions`, or from the same hook as a headless component in `packages/core/src/ha/headless.tsx` (`<HaLight entityId>{(light) => …}</HaLight>`), for a list of entities. `./testing` (`packages/core/src/testing.tsx`) is for tests of connected cards: a fake transport and a render under a real store. `./demo` is the demo layer: the base transports in `packages/core/src/demo.ts`, and one file per domain plus `house.ts` (which puts them together as one house) in `packages/core/src/demo/`.
- `packages/dashboard/src/` — `@dashhaus/dashboard`, what every skin's Lovelace card draws on, behind six entries: `index.ts` for the Lovelace card (consumers import `@dashhaus/dashboard`), `skin.ts` for the frozen skin-author runtime (consumers import `@dashhaus/dashboard/skin`: `LovelaceDashboardElement`, `registerCard`, `scopeToHost`, `autoInput`, `useActionStatus`, `LovelaceHass` and `AutoEntityEntry`), `card.tsx` for the card frame every skin draws (consumers import `@dashhaus/dashboard/card`; footprint flags, ids and aria, details state, and the tile/sheet composition, with the skin's own markup and CSS outside it), `schema.ts` for the YAML reader and card schema (consumers import `@dashhaus/dashboard/schema`), `layout.ts` for the automatic layout (consumers import `@dashhaus/dashboard/layout`) and `conformance.ts` for the generic card preset. The Lovelace entry: `element.tsx`, the custom-element base (shadow root, one store per frontend connection, the React root, the skin's web font linked from the page), `actionMessage.ts` (the words for a rejected command), `useActionStatus.ts` (the store's latest rejected command with Retry and Dismiss, for a skin's recovery row; frozen in `./skin`), `nav.ts` and `useDashboardNav.ts` (a Lovelace card's bottom nav: its destinations, their labels, and what each tap does), `rooms.ts` (re-exports an automatic layout's room sensors from `layout.ts`) and `styles.ts` (tokens onto the shadow host). It imports `@dashhaus/core`, never a skin or a stylesheet. The pure entries: `schema.ts` (the `Reader` every config extends, with problems at their paths, "did you mean", and `nav`/`pages`; `readCard`/`readCards` over a kind table with a per-kind `options` escape hatch; `renderCard` dispatch; the shared colour, unit, sensor, weather, alarm and Owlet option readers) and `layout.ts` (visibility, area resolution, name sort, `groupByArea`, `placeSection` and `roomSensors`). They use no React, no DOM, no skin and no Node builtin, so `@dashhaus/deploy` and a Node config can import them. The skin entry re-exports the element base and `styles.ts`; the main entry keeps its broader nav and layout surface.
- `packages/fake-home-assistant/` — `@dashhaus/fake-home-assistant`, the private Node-only fake Home Assistant the `dx/` journeys and tests deploy into. It is plain ESM with no runtime dependency and no build step: `src/house.js` (the sample house and its registries), `src/fake-home-assistant.js` (`FakeHomeAssistant`, `connectTo`), `src/server.js` (`startFakeHomeAssistant`: the websocket API, REST reads, `/local/`, `/api/fake/state` and the dashboard page), `src/cli.js` and `bin.js` (the `fake-home-assistant` command), with hand-written `.d.ts` declarations; `src/index.js` is its single root export. It owns a Node Vitest project and its own `tsconfig.json`, is workspace-linked so `verify/dx/` imports the specifier, and is not a release package: it is absent from `scripts/release/manifest.js`, `release:pack` and the fixed version set. README: the API, the commands it answers, injection and its known limits.
- `packages/deploy/src/` — `@dashhaus/deploy`, the Node-only deploy CLI (`bin.js`, installed as `dashhaus-deploy`). `config.js` is the pure author config surface a `dashboard.config.mjs` imports as `@dashhaus/deploy/config`: `DeployConfig`, `DeployDashboard`, `DeployHa`, `DeployCard`, `GeneratedSkin` and `generatedDashboards`, with no import and no React; its declarations are `config.d.ts`. `load.js` bundles the user's config with esbuild (TypeScript or JavaScript, a path outside this repo included), imports it, applies the `.env` overrides each dashboard names and holds paths to Home Assistant's hyphen and uniqueness rules. `deploy.js` runs the config's optional `build()` with the config's directory as cwd, hashes and uploads the bundle it names (over SSH or into a mounted folder), registers or updates the dashboard resource, and reconciles dashboards: create the missing ones once, never overwrite a layout, follow a sidebar title change. `cli.js` parses `--config`, `--skip-build` and `--help`, reads `.env` beside the config and prints the result. It imports no `@dashhaus/core`, `@dashhaus/dashboard` or skin; `verify/deploy-package.test.ts` holds that boundary, the config surface's purity, the repo config's generated layout import and the copied-install check.
- `packages/bundle/` — `@dashhaus/bundle`, the prebuilt one-file Lovelace bundle. `index.js` is the workspace entry (source condition) and exports `bundlePath` as a file URL; `dist/index.js` is the packed entry, beside `dist/dashhaus.js`. `npm run build:ha` writes the bundle and `release:pack` builds the package `dist`; the package has no runtime dependencies, so a deploy-only app installs no React or core. README: install with `@dashhaus/deploy`, name `fileURLToPath(bundlePath)`, run `dashhaus-deploy --skip-build`.
- `packages/create-dashhaus-skin/` — `create-dashhaus-skin`, the standalone skin scaffolder (`bin.js`, `src/index.js`, `src/templates/` copied to `dist/`). It writes cards, adapters, a YAML reader, the element, a local preview on a sample house (`npm run preview`, `npm run snapshot` for PNGs through Playwright, which it doesn't install), `npm test` (`tsc`, the conformance preset and the CSS rules), a Vite config that emits one JS file, a deploy config with `.env.example`, and an `AGENTS.md` (with a `CLAUDE.md` importing it): the one document a consumer's agent reads, so it is agent-first and changes with the templates (`spec/build-a-skin.md`). `--tarballs <dir>` points the project at release tarballs. No Storybook, no workspace and no repository path. `scripts/release/verify.js` runs the packed bin, and `verify/dx/author.js` takes the whole journey from the tarball.
- `src/skins/<skin>/` — one whole UI. ### Adding a skin

Run `npm run new-skin -- <lower-case-kebab-name>`; for example, `npm run new-skin -- canvas`. It validates every registration and the package name (`@dashhaus/<skin>`, reserved names refused) before it creates a private workspace package at `src/skins/<skin>`, linked by `npm install` through the `src/skins/*` glob; add domains with `npm run new-card -- light --skin canvas`. Existing Orb and mature Field keep their specialized generator adapters. `--study` makes a study instead: a skin an agent built to see what the generators make, kept to learn from and never shipped (`npm run new-skin -- <name> --study`; spec/skins.md → Studies). The workflow, namespaces and rules are [spec/skins.md](spec/skins.md); a standalone skin in its own directory, from the published packages with no Storybook or workspace, is `npm create dashhaus-skin@latest my-skin` ([spec/build-a-skin.md](spec/build-a-skin.md)).

### Adding an existing domain to another skin

The domain and Home Assistant slice already exist; add only the new skin's drawing and adapters. For field, `npm run new-card -- <name> --skin field` does steps 2 to 5: it reads the domain's types, `useHa<Name>` and handlers, copies orb's demo seeds into the card's own `demo/`, and draws a **control** (one key over `onToggle`, the default when the domain has actions) or a **reading** (glyph and today, the default when it has none; `--kind reading` for a control domain whose keys you'll add by hand). It refuses, saying why, a domain whose hook needs an option it can't know (a required option in `Ha<Name>Options`) or whose handlers have no `onToggle` without `--kind reading`. `verify/field-conformance.test.ts` then holds the card to these steps, hand-built or generated. Then make it the real thing: its screen, its glyph and pictogram in `icons.tsx`, its sections in `spec/field.md` and `spec/cards.md`.

1. Reuse `packages/core/src/entities/<name>/` and `useHa<Name>` from `packages/core/src/ha/hooks/<name>.ts`. Do not fork its entity model, mapper, actions, history parsing or demo transport for a skin.
2. Add the presentational component, skin-prefixed CSS, icons, local demo seeds, interaction tests and stories under `src/skins/<skin>/cards/<name>/`, its stories titled `<Skin>/Cards/<Name>` with the standard set (Storybook below). Presentational files never import `@dashhaus/core`'s Home Assistant subpaths; keep worst-case data in the demo seeds rather than adding product-only debug props or URL switches.
3. Add `src/skins/<skin>/ha/Ha<Name><Surface>.tsx`, where `<Surface>` is that skin's noun such as `Tile` or `Module`. It calls the shared `useHa<Name>` hook and only adapts its result to the presentational component. Add its real-store Storybook story, titled `<Skin>/Connected/<Name>`, and focused adapter tests.
4. Extend that skin's Lovelace parser, generated layout and renderer, plus the corresponding YAML in `spec/deploy.md`. Reuse the connected container there instead of repeating hook-to-view wiring. In field: the card's domains in `FIELD_KINDS` (an `id` pattern, or `card:`, picks it on a domain another card draws) and its connected module in `LIVE`.
5. Export the presentational component, connected container and their prop types from the skin's entry (`src/skins/<skin>/index.ts`); token CSS is imported once per skin, by that entry and not by individual cards.
6. Run the skin's rules test, `npm run check`, `npm run test-storybook` and `npm run build:ha`; visually inspect the state matrix, every supported width and footprint, compact rows, details, unavailable data and the longest labels.

## Storybook

Every skin's section has the same groups, in this order, so the tree has one shape whichever skin you open; Bundles alone is orb's. `.storybook/preview.tsx`'s `storySort` lists them and `verify/storybook.test.ts` checks both. A study (spec/skins.md → Studies) sits under a last top-level **Studies** section as `Studies/<Name>`, and opens on a **Read me first** page drawn from its README before the same groups.

1. **Cards** — `<Skin>/Cards/<Name>`, one per card. Every card has the same stories under the same names: its own states first, then **Unavailable**, **Worst case** (its `<NAME>_WORST_CASE` or worst-case seed), **All states**, **Sizes**, **Footprints**, **Compact**, **Details**, **Details, unavailable** and **Details, phone**. A card adds stories for what only it can do (a climate range, a colour lamp, a view-only alarm) beside the one they extend, and drops none.
2. **Bundles** — orb only: its joined, lead and titled bundles. A bundle is an orb idea, not a gap in other skins, so they leave the group out.
3. **Dashboards** — whole pages and their furniture: every skin's hand-composed `Examples` (phone, tablet, wall panel), its `Lovelace card`, its whole dashboard drawn from YAML on the demo transports, its `Header` and its `Nav bar`. The examples show ways to compose a dashboard, not a register of cards: a card belongs in one only when it shows something about composing.
4. **Foundations** — a skin's rules across cards, first its **Design rules**: an MDX page that draws the skin's spec (orb: `spec/orb.md`; field: `spec/field.md`) through `.storybook/blocks/MarkdownWithStories.tsx`, with chosen stories after their sections. The markdown stays the source, so edit it, not the page; every section shows unless the page skips it by heading. Then each skin's palette and **Parts** (its parts from `shared/`, each across its states, drawn with the skin's own components and CSS); orb's colour temperature, compact rows and worst case.
5. **Connected** — one story per card under the card's name: its connected card on the demo transports, so a press goes through the `useHa<Name>` hook, mapper and actions and the state comes back through the store. These live in the skin's `ha/`, apart from `Cards`, because only `ha/` and `lovelace/` may import `@dashhaus/core`'s Home Assistant subpaths.

Any other group a skin can't fill holds one **Not in <Skin>** story that says why, never filler. Today every skin fills every group it has.

### Primitives

`packages/core/src/primitives/` has no stories, and shouldn't. A primitive is behaviour without markup, so a story would have to invent markup and styles that belong to no skin, and what it showed would be wrong in the ways that matter: a track's scroll-or-set on touch depends on the skin's `touch-action: pan-y`, a press on its `:active` feedback, the sheet's motion on its CSS. That story would pass axe and still prove nothing about either skin. The contracts are pinned by the unit tests beside each hook (`press.test.tsx`, `slider.test.tsx`, `sheet.test.tsx`, `keypad.test.tsx`), and every skin exercises them in its card stories: `usePress` on any card's orb or key, `useSlider` on Light → Sizes, `useRangeSlider` on Climate → Heat/cool range (orb) or Auto range (field), `useSheet` on any card's Details, phone, `useKeypad` on Alarm → Details, keypad. Feel a primitive there, on a phone. If a third skin ever wants to try one before it has cards, revisit this.

## Hard rules

For every skin, the rules in spec/skins.md → Rules every skin keeps: one card, one model (a direct one Home Assistant entity, or a composed one with the companions its hook composes); colours come from tokens (no colour literal in a skin's CSS); a card's height never changes with its state; motion only on `transform`, `opacity`, `clip-path` and colours, with easing from tokens; hover only under `@media (hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)`; a theme attribute of its own (never the shared `data-theme`); controls feel like controls (no selection or tap highlight, `touch-action: manipulation`, press feedback on `:active`). That section is their one home; the scaffold's `AGENTS.md` repeats them for consumers, and `verify/skin-rules.test.ts` keeps the two in step.

For the orb skin:

- The accent is `--brand-c` / `--brand-h`; easing comes from the `--ease-*` tokens; a card's height comes from the shared `footprint` matrix plus container width.
- One UI language (spec/orb.md → One UI language): a raised `--btn` is tapped, a recessed `--groove` is slid or set, a readout has no container; solid accent is on for every role; a dashed outline is unavailable and nothing else. No hatching, no second strength of accent, no default orb colour.
- No expanding cards: more detail opens in a sheet, from a tap on the card's name (`CardTitle`) or a press and hold. Cards carry no open button.
- Prefix each card's CSS classes with its card code: `lw-` light, `cw-` climate, `sw-` sensor, `al-` alarm, `cv-` cover, `tw-` toggle, `wx-` weather, `ow-` owlet sock, `hu-` humidifier, `sn-` sound<!-- new-card: orb codes -->. Outside the cards, `hh-` is the dashboard header, `hb-` the bundles and `hd-` the shared card system.
- Attention follows the spec/orb.md glow contract and the `attention-budget` skill: glow is never the only state channel, damping follows semantic urgency not card position, and urgent states get a warn marker rather than more brightness.
- Every compact row carries a domain icon, primary identity and one state/action cue; colour never defines a row.

## Adding a card

```sh
npm run new-card -- fan --code fn --domain fan                                  # a control: on/off, over fan.toggle
npm run new-card -- co2 --code co --domain sensor --kind reading --unit ppm     # a reading: read-only number
# add --dry-run to preview
```

Pick the kind by what the card does: a **control** (default) is an orb and a pill that switch the entity; a **reading** is a bare icon and a readout that only show it, with no services. Either way it writes a working card, Home Assistant side included, and registers it everywhere: `src/skins/orb/index.ts`, `packages/core/src/index.ts`, the fixture test, the headless components, `spec/cards.md`, the prefix list above, and the orb skin's Lovelace card (`src/skins/orb/lovelace/`, `spec/deploy.md`). It passes `npm run check` and `npm run test-storybook` before you touch it. Then make it the real thing:

1. `packages/core/src/entities/<name>/types.ts`: the fields the card shows. `src/skins/orb/cards/<name>/icons.tsx`: its domain icon.
2. `<Name>Card.tsx`: the card. It is built from the shared card system in `src/skins/orb/shared/ui.tsx`: `useCard(code, …)` gives the `article` attributes, footprint flags and ids; an `hd-head` holds the `Orb`, the `CardTitle` (given `onOpen` outside the sheet) and, in the sheet only, `SheetClose`; then one `hd-slot` and the sheet blocks. Spread `paint(role, glow, own, prop)` onto the article for its colour role, glow and colour slot. A reading's figures go in a `Readout`, laid out with the shared `hd-bar`, `hd-metric`, `hd-label` and `hd-none` classes. `detailsSheet()` and `detailsTile()` build the sheet and the tile, so compact, standard and featured footprints plus the sheet come for free.
3. `packages/core/src/ha/mappers/<name>.ts` and, for a control, `packages/core/src/ha/actions/<name>.ts`: the raw attributes and the native services, through `serviceRunner()` (`home-assistant-integration` skill). Keep the fixture's `unknown` and `unavailable` entities. `packages/core/src/ha/hooks/<name>.ts` (`useHa<Name>`) wires them to the store; any companion entities or options belong there, and `src/skins/orb/ha/Ha<Name>Tile.tsx` only passes them through.
4. Leave the example dashboards (`src/skins/orb/dashboards/Examples.stories.tsx`) alone unless the card shows something about composing a page they don't: they are examples, not a list of every card. Its own stories show it on its own, and the Lovelace card's dashboard shows it among the others from YAML.
5. Finish its section in `spec/cards.md`, and add it to `spec/home-assistant.md`'s slice list.
6. Finish its Lovelace card in `src/skins/orb/lovelace/`: its YAML keys (its `KIND_SPECS` entry in `config.ts`, its `TILES` row in `ConfigDashboard.tsx`) and their rows in `spec/deploy.md`, and its place in `auto.ts`. A domain has one default card in `DOMAIN_KIND`; another card on the same domain goes in `SHARED_KINDS`, picked with `card:` or by an entity-id pattern. The generator places only a control on a new domain in `auto.ts`. A hand-built card needs all of this.

`verify/orb-conformance.test.ts` holds every card, generated or hand-built, to the files, card system, exports, docs and Home Assistant connection above, and to the generic preset for its story section, class codes and imports, and says which step is missing; `verify/storybook.test.ts` holds its stories to the standard set. The generated stories already have it: replace the seeds, keeping `unavailable` and the worst case in `<NAME>_WORST_CASE`. Then verify per the section above.
