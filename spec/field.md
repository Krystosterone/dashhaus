# Field skin

A second skin, `src/skins/field/`, drawn after Teenage Engineering's EP–133 K.O. II: a flat light-grey body, printed labels, flat-topped keys, a thin fader with a round thumb, and a dark segment-style screen. It sits on the same entity models and Home Assistant hooks as orb (`useHaLight` and the rest); only the drawing differs. Stories are under **Field** in Storybook, in the same groups as orb's except Bundles, which are orb's alone, with a **Finish** toolbar (Auto, Alloy, Black). Foundations → Design rules draws this file with live stories beside its sections (edit it here, not there), Foundations → Palette shows the parts below and what each means, Foundations → Parts each part across its states; under Dashboards, Examples compose a page per device, Lovelace card is its whole dashboard from YAML, Header is the top of every page and Nav bar its bottom.

Its Lovelace card, `custom:dashhaus-field`, and its YAML are in [deploy.md](deploy.md#the-field-dashboard).

## Design rules

The every-skin hard rules in AGENTS.md apply. `src/skins/field/styles/rules.test.ts` checks the ones below that CSS can show.

- **Three parts, one meaning each.**
  - **Key** (flat-topped, just raised): press it (`usePress`, shared with orb). A key that can't be pressed lies flat, in dim ink; the chosen key of a group (a mode, a preset) stays down, pressed in, in full ink, so the current choice never reads as out of reach. Its glyph is the domain's (a bulb for a light), so the key is also the row's identity.
  - **Fader** (a printed legend with a rule out to its end, a thin dark slot, a dotted scale, a dot per stop when it has ten or fewer, else in tens, a round thumb): slide or set along it. A tap jumps the thumb; a drag follows the finger (`useSlider`, shared with orb). Where what it sets reports no level (a light or a fan while off), it draws no thumb, since any place would claim a level Home Assistant never reported, yet a tap or a slide still sets one there and turns it on. A fader that sets a loudness or an output (a volume) lights only while that output is active and unmuted (cards.md → What every card shows → A level that is a loudness); unlit, its legend gives the reason before the value (`Off · 62%`, `Muted · 45%`, `Paused · 30%`), and setting it never switches the output on.
  - **Screen** (always dark, in both finishes): read it, never press it. A pictogram and a big thin figure on the left, up to two meta lines on the right.
- **A picture is a window in the screen** (`Picture`, Field's media surface): a camera's still or a player's artwork in the screen's own dark well, clipped to a fixed box, with no bezel and no words on it; the screen's meta lines say how old a still is. Before a frame, with none to keep and while unavailable, it is the screen with the card's pictogram unlit, never dashed. It is data, so it never inverts with an alarm (severity stays in the screen's words), carries no parameter colour, and never sits in a row, whose key or glyph names it. Pressed, it opens the live stream or the panel; the module's name still opens the panel, as on every module, and while unavailable it takes no press or focus. Its lifecycle is core's (core.md → Pictures).
- **A reading has nothing to press.** Where a control has its key, a reading has its **glyph**: the domain's glyph on a plate that lies flat, as a key that can't be pressed does, so the row keeps its identity in the same place (`Glyph`; not a button, no LED). Where a control has its fader, a reading has **today**: a printed legend with the day's low and high over a strip of screen, one column per hour, in the fader's 44 px (`Today`). Hours not yet come are dark, unless the model knows them (a calendar's plan): from `aheadFrom` on they draw their level in the dim screen ink, the column dimmed as a dark hour's, so a plan never reads as a past, as orb's forecast sticks do. Only the name opens its details.
- **A lit LED says on.** The LED in a key's corner is the one sign that something is on (`--fd-led-lit`); the screen agrees, with a lit pictogram and a bright figure, or `OFF` in the dim screen ink. On is the model's active set (cards.md → What every card shows → One active signal), the same set Orb's accent follows, and the LED, the pictogram and the figure follow it together: a transient state between two active ones (a player buffering between tracks) keeps them lit, so they don't flicker, and a powered on with nothing playing (a TV showing an input) is lit.
- **Colour names a parameter**, the way a knob's colour names what it sets on TE's devices. `--fd-param-a`, orange, is a module's first parameter (a light's brightness; climate's single target or lower heat threshold). `--fd-param-b`, blue, is its second (a light's warmth; climate's upper cool threshold). The parameter colour appears on its fader thumb and screen pictogram, not as a heat/cool signal; words and mode geometry carry that meaning. A reading sets nothing, so it has no parameter colour: its pictogram lights in the figure's ink, and its meter and an activity's (climate's runtime) are neutral.
- **The screen speaks in fixed places**, like a segment display. Numbers keep their digit count, and the leading digits are unlit segments (`080%`), drawn by CSS so screen readers hear "80%". A pictogram is always there unlit, and lights up to its level from the bottom; a light's bulb keeps its base lit while on, so 3% still reads lit. A missing reading appears as width-specific blank segments (`---` or `----`), never a plausible number. A word on the screen takes four places at most, no wider than `OPEN`, and the same word in every footprint: a card hand-picks its figures (the lock's `LOCK`, `OPEN`, `JAM`), a state that fits none is `ON` or `OFF` with the model's word on the first meta line, and the figure's type never steps down to fit a fifth place. Only an alarm on a standard module may take five (`ALARM`, `SMOKE`); a row's alarm says how long ago instead (`NOW`, `3m`), or blank segments when it doesn't know. A clock is `HH:MM` on 24 hours; on a 12-hour dashboard (spec/cards.md → Clock words follow the dashboard's hour cycle) it keeps the same four places, its first an unlit segment before 10 (`2:00` beside `12:00`, `Digits` with `clockParts`), as the header's does, and the period is said on the meta line in core's words (`Tomorrow 2:00 PM`), never on the screen and never by stepping the figure's type down. Blank segments mean the screen can't name it (unavailable, no reading yet, a transition, an integration's own state in words), and the meta line always does; a figure the device doesn't declare isn't drawn at all (a panel's channel for it goes, a meta line leaves it out), and where the screen's fixed places must still hold one, its blank segments say core's `Not reported`, never a phrase of the card's own (cards.md → What every card says → A blank figure says whether it may never come); a known resting state is never blank, but its figure in the dim ink (`SHUT`, `DOCK`, `FREE`). One hook draws every dim figure: the card passes its module `rest` for a resting state, or for readings that stopped (an Owlet's), and off always rests; `ModuleFrame` sets `data-rest` on the module and its panel, and one rule in `module.css` draws the figure, and the panel's second channel, in `--fd-screen-ink-2`. A card never dims its figure in its own CSS (`styles/rules.test.ts`).
- **A clock runs on by itself**, as a segment display's does (cards.md → What every card shows): worked out once and left to CSS, its places strips of digits on the screen, so nothing re-renders every second. It cuts and never slides (hard cuts, the keyframe stops written to four decimals); it stops at the end of what it measures and holds there; it starts from the fractional position, each place that far into its turn, so it turns when the rule beside it says it should; reduced motion keeps it, since a hard cut is not motion; and a dashboard's `quiet` draws none, neither the clock nor the rule that fills with it (a player's position: Media player below).
- **Details open in a panel**, never by growing the module: tap the name (it carries a small arrow) or press and hold the key. The panel is the module's plate grown into a dialog: a centred panel on wider screens, a bottom sheet on phones that its header drags down to close (`useSheet`, shared with orb). Its screen is taller and carries a second channel on its right. Under the primary controls come capability-specific groups under printed legends, today as a 24-hour meter on a strip of screen, then facts in small print.
- **Severity is how much of the screen inverts**, never a colour. Field has no warning colour and no glow to raise; its screen is always dark, so a light one is found before anything is read, in both finishes and with colour removed. Each card ranks its warnings (`packages/core/src/entities/severity.ts`), so a door left open never looks like a baby's oxygen alarm:
  - **Note**, a choice rather than a fault (a baby monitor's base station switched off): plain words in place of the area, without the marker (`Module`'s `note`); a warning takes its place.
  - **Advisory**, look when convenient (a low battery, a door left open, the weather's warnings): an outlined warning sign before its words, said as "Warning: …" (`Warn`). One about the device rather than its reading is printed on the plate in place of the area, where it has the module's full width in either footprint (`Module`'s `warning`); one about the reading stays on the screen.
  - **Urgent**, act soon, something has stopped working or is about to (a sock off, readings that stopped, an alarm's entry delay): the same words in an inverted block, on the screen's first line, or a row's line, which has no screen lines (`Warn`'s `block`); the figure stays.
  - **Alarm**, act now, safety (a vitals alert, a triggered alarm): the whole screen inverts, with a bezel in the screen's own dark so it stays a screen on the light alloy plate. The figure becomes the alarm (`79% O₂`, `ALARM`) and its first line goes a size up and bold; an ink rule lines the plate's edge; the LED blinks. It flashes once a second (under WCAG's three a second, a hard cut like a segment display's) for the first ten seconds after it started, then holds inverted until it clears; timed from the alarm's start where the card knows it (an alarm's last change, the first vitals alert sensor's), so a module drawn later, a view switched back to or a reconnect, picks the flash up where it would be and never replays it: steady from the start under reduced motion, or on a dashboard set `quiet` (a nursery's panel at night). A screen reader hears it once as it starts. An advisory on the same module steps aside; the panel still lists it.
  - **The alert line**: every urgent and alarm warning on the dashboard, on a strip of screen under its title, so one below the fold still shows on a phone (`AlertLine`, fed by `ModuleFrame`'s `alert`). Alarms first, then the newest; each entry its level, boxed with its glyph and word (an alarm's box inverted, blinking by opacity in phase with its module, so its off beat never looks like an urgent box), then the module's name and what is wrong, a 44 px row whose tap opens the module's details; three show, then "+N more". With nothing pressing, it isn't drawn. Foundations → Alerts shows the ladder.
- **Unavailable** blanks the screen, says `UNAVAILABLE` and when it was last seen on the meta line, and lays every shown control flat; a key keeps its place but drops its state word, since the state isn't known, and stays focusable (`aria-disabled`, `usePress`'s `unavailable`, as orb's orb does), so a hold still opens its details. A plus or minus step at its bound lies flat the same way, so focus never drops off it. It never fabricates a reading or target. Today's legend says `Unavailable`, not since when, as the screen already says when it was last seen; a sensor or weather keeps the low and high it read instead. The meter keeps what it drew, the hours it was gone dashed.
- **Type** is Archivo, a geometric sans, set wide (`--fd-wide`), upper-case for printed labels and names, and light for the screen's figure. Names are upper-cased by CSS, so the accessible name keeps its case. Nothing printed is smaller than 10 px, so a wall panel's labels read from arm's length: legends, facts and the words under keys at 10 px, the area and the screen's meta lines at 10.5 px. `shared/surface.ts` names Archivo's stylesheet: the Lovelace card links it from the page, and Storybook loads it before a Field story renders (the setup's `fontHref`); without it the stack falls back to the system sans.
- **Keys** latch or act once. A latching key (power, a colour) has an LED; a key that acts once (a quick level) has none, so a lit LED only ever means on: the lamp, or the colour it is showing.
- **A key's word is what pressing it does** wherever that isn't the plain inverse of on and off. A plain on/off key (a light, a mode, a sound) may print `On` or `Off`, since its LED makes the latch plain and it is announced as pressed. Any other key prints its verb (`Turn on`, `Turn off`, `Close`, `Open`, `Stop`) and says it in its label, so it isn't also announced as pressed; its LED still says what is on or open, and the screen says the state or mode.
- **A lamp's own colour is data, not a signal.** The `--fd-lamp-*` tokens appear only as swatches: a square on each colour key, and the lens pictogram on the screen while the lamp shows that colour. The names and the hue each covers are shared with orb (`packages/core/src/entities/light/color.ts`); each skin draws its own swatches.
- **Footprints:** `standard` is a 160 px module (header, screen, a 44 px control row); `compact` is a 64 px row (key, name and area, a fixed-width screen). The height never changes with state or entity type, nor with whether the module has an area. Container queries simplify the inside at narrow widths, the same for every card (`module.css`): under 240 px a module keeps its figure and one meta line and its pictogram goes, since the key or glyph below names the device; under 280 px a row's screen narrows and its pictogram goes.
- **Scoped tokens.** Every custom property starts with `--fd-`, including the easings (`--fd-ease-out`, `--fd-ease-in-out`), so field and orb can share a page. No colour outside `styles/tokens.css`. The page is a plain body, with no pattern.
- **Motion:** presses take `--fd-press` (120 ms) on `transform` and `box-shadow`; a fader jump moves its rail with `transform`; the pictogram fills with `clip-path`; the LED fades its colour; an Owlet sock's heart flashes its colour while readings arrive; an alarm's screen swaps its two inks once a second for ten seconds; a player's position lights a rule along the screen's foot (`clip-path`) and turns its clock's places with hard cuts, each a linear animation started where it is, so nothing re-renders every second (A clock runs on by itself, above; orb's is spec/orb.md → A clock runs on by itself). Reduced motion keeps colour and fill changes and drops the movement, turns the heart's flash into a slow swell, and holds an alarm's screen inverted without flashing. Hover only under `(hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)`.

## Dashboards

A field page is `FieldPage` (`src/skins/field/dashboards/layout.tsx`): its header, the alert line, its areas, then its nav docked at the bottom. The Lovelace card draws the same page from its YAML, so the examples and the real card can't drift apart.

- **The header is print, the clock a screen.** The page's name printed large and upper-case, today's date under it in the area's small print, and, given `showTime`, the time on a strip of screen as tall as a control row, its figure the module screen's (`Header`). It is page furniture, not a module: no plate, no key, nothing to press, the clock the one thing lit. The digits keep their places, a 12-hour clock's first one an unlit segment before 10, so 9:05 and 12:59 are the same width and nothing shifts as the minute turns. Date and time come from `packages/core/src/entities/clock.ts`, shared with orb's header.
- **The alert line sits under the header**, and is the one thing that moves the page: an alarm stays in its module's ordinary place, and the line names it on the first screen.
- **An area is a legend and a grid** (`Area`): its name printed with the fader legend's rule, a note at its end, its modules in columns from its own width: as many 260 px tracks as fit (300 px for rows). A page with one area prints no legend. An area's `columns` is a most, never a count, so a module never narrows below its own width to make a row; its gaps are the footprint's (12 px between modules, 8 px between rows), never set. `Areas` puts areas side by side, one column each, for a hand-composed tablet or wall panel; under 640 px of page they stack. A YAML row is an area: the automatic layout's are Home Assistant's areas, and `rows` titles its own (spec/deploy.md → The format).
- **A width takes tracks the row has.** A module's `width` widens it across that many of its row's tracks where the row is wide enough to have them, and across all of them where it isn't (the shared layout clamps it), so it never makes a track of its own. Its height stays its footprint's; only its screen and fader get longer. `column` starts it in a column while the row has the tracks it was written for.
- **Mix footprints by column, never by row.** A row of one footprint is a grid whose rows are all one height. A row that mixes footprints stacks each column's modules (every module given a `column`): 12 px between modules, 8 px between two rows, the stacks top-aligned or, with `align: bottom`, set on the row's foot. A short module never sits beside a tall one in a grid row, and with fewer tracks than columns the stacks wrap in column order.
- **The nav is a plate of keys at the bottom** (`NavBar`): the module's plate, docked as the page scrolls, holding a key per destination (an area of this page, a page of the card's own, another dashboard), its word printed on it. Where you are is the key that stays down, sunk to the plate's face, as a group's chosen key does: no LED, since a place isn't on, and no colour, since it sets nothing. A tap still answers, an area's jumping back to its top. A key that leaves the dashboard carries a small arrow. Keys keep their widths while they fit, then narrow to their longest word, a two-word name taking two lines, so a phone holds four; a row that still doesn't fit scrolls under the plate's edge, the cut key saying there is more, the current one kept in view. It is page furniture, not a module: nothing on it reads a state. A page with one destination has none; so does a wall panel, unless its YAML lists one. A rejected command rides the dock above it.
- **Far is type, never size** (`viewing="far"`, YAML `viewing: far`): a wall panel read from across the room sets its modules' print a step up (names 14 px, the area and screen lines, legends and key words 12 px, the alert line 13 px) and its screens' figures a weight up, from light to regular, since thin strokes vanish at a distance. Heights don't move; the leading tightens instead, so the far page fits where the near one did, and longer words truncate a little sooner. Width can't tell a wall panel from a tablet in the hand, so far is never inferred. A detail panel is opened up close, so it keeps the near type.
- **Mix footprints by column, not by row.** A column of a wall panel leads with whole modules and takes 64 px rows under them; a row of whole modules stays whole, so screens line up across it.

## From the exploration and the references

Kept selectively from the exploration (`ha_01_universal_home_assistant_field_console.html`): the alloy and black finishes, module plate, keys with LEDs, dark screen and orange parameter. Climate also keeps the useful current-temperature-first readout and compact mode key bank; its normalized entity capabilities, not the prototype's fixed console, decide what appears.

Changed after looking at TE's own product photos (OP–1 field, OP–XY, TX–6, EP–133 K.O. II): monospace became a wide geometric sans; orange moved from "on" to naming a parameter; keys and plates lost most of their depth; the striped fader thumb became a round one in a thin slot; the screen gained a pictogram; the fader gained a printed legend; the perforated page became plain.

Left out: screws, scanlines, speaker grilles, entity ids as labels, blur crossfades and staggered entry, click sounds, and the console chrome (banks, patch bay, command bus).

## Light

- **Module:** the power key (bulb glyph, LED), the screen (a bulb pictogram filled with the brightness, its base lit whenever the lamp is on so 3% still reads lit; the figure; its warmth, or its colour's name; since when), and the brightness fader. On/off lamps: one wide key with its state word, no fader. Compact: key, name and area, screen.
- **Panel:** the screen with a second channel: the lamp's colour while it shows one (a lens in that colour and its name, "Amber"), otherwise its warmth (sun pictogram, kelvin, its name: "Warm white"; blank segments, and its fader, saying `No reading yet` while a lamp with a colour temperature mode reports none, as while it is off), and no warmth for a lamp without one. Then the power key and brightness fader; quick levels 5, 25, 50, 100 (Night, Dim, Half, Full); the warmth fader in blue, flat while the lamp is off; a colour lamp's seven colour keys (Red, Amber, Yellow, Green, Blue, Purple, White), the one it shows latched, and choosing one turns it on, four and three on a phone so each name fits; today; last changed (or last seen) and what the lamp can do. A lamp with both shows a warmth or a colour, never both, as Home Assistant reports it.
- **Unavailable:** blank segments, "Unavailable" and when it was last seen, on the panel's screen too; the key spans the row with no state word and the brightness fader goes, since the brightness isn't known; the warmth fader draws no thumb, and the quick levels and colour keys lie flat.
- **Off:** Home Assistant reports no brightness while a light is off, so the fader stays with no thumb, said as "Off" and never as a level it was last set to; a tap or a slide turns the light on there. A lit light that hasn't reported a brightness yet has no thumb either, and is said as "On".
- **Unknown:** reachable but not reported yet (a group before its first state, an optimistic light before its first command), so it can be driven: blank segments and "Unknown" with since when, never OFF; the key and the empty fader act as on an unlit light, an on/off lamp's key printing `Unknown`.

## Climate

- **Module:** the thermostat key switches the entity and its LED is the on signal. The screen leads with room temperature, with target and HVAC action secondary. Heat, cool and the other modes use distinct geometry and words, never colour. Heat, cool and auto put their target on one orange fader; under 240 px, where the screen keeps one meta line, it keeps what the unit is doing, since the fader's legend prints the target. `heat_cool` uses an ordered two-thumb fader: orange is “heat below”, blue is “cool above”, and its native sliders never cross. Off becomes one wide **Turn on** key; dry and fan-only have no temperature target, so their key spans the row as **Turn off**, the mode on the screen, its pictogram lit in the figure's ink since no parameter is set. The key is a round thermostat's dial, so a column tells it from a room sensor's thermometer. Compact is the same 64 px key/name/screen row as light.
- **Panel:** the second screen channel holds the target while on, or the last room reading while off (`No reading yet` before the first; none at all, the screen keeping its meta lines, for a thermostat that never reports one). On, with no room reading, the module's figure is blank segments that say `No reading yet`, or `Not reported` for a thermostat with no `current_temperature`. It repeats the primary control, adds exact plus/minus steps for a single target (**Fine target**, one step of the entity's at a time) or a heat/cool range (**Fine range**), and draws only the entity's supported HVAC modes, presets and fan modes as keys. The current choice is printed in the legend and its key stays down, pressed in; it stays focusable and is announced as pressed (`Key`'s `chosen`), and pressing it again does nothing. No choice key gets a second “on” LED. Today uses the neutral screen ink because runtime is status, not a parameter. When it last changed (or was last seen), then the room's humidity and the supported temperature range, finish the panel (`Last changed 10:31`, `Humidity 41% · 7–30°C`).
- **Unavailable:** blank segments and “Unavailable”, one flat key across the row with no state word, no fabricated temperature or target, and a neutral unavailable history meter when history exists.

## Sensor

- **Module:** a reading. The thermometer glyph on its flat plate, then today's temperature meter with its low and high, where light and climate have their key and fader. The screen leads with the temperature, its thermometer filling from the bulb (lit to the bulb at −10 °C, to the top at 40 °C) in the figure's ink. The two meta lines take, most important first: the humidity (`44% RH`), comfort in words ("Cool, dry"; none outdoors), and whether it is rising or falling. A low battery (15% or less) takes the area's line under the name, behind the warning marker. A sensor with only humidity makes humidity the figure, behind a drop. Under 240 px the screen keeps the figure and one meta line, and the pictogram goes, since the glyph below names the reading. Compact: glyph, name and area (or the low battery), screen.
- **Panel:** humidity on the screen's second channel (a drop filled to the humidity), or the module's meta lines for a sensor without it. Then today's temperature and today's humidity, each with its low and high; then the small print: when it last reported, then its battery (`Battery 82%`, or behind the marker `Battery 8%, replace soon`).
- **Unavailable:** blank segments, "Unavailable" and when it was last seen, and the hours it was gone dashed on the meters; today's low and high stay, since they were read. A sensor that has never reported says "No reading yet" over blank segments.

## Weather

- **Module:** a reading. The sun-and-cloud glyph on its flat plate, then today's temperature meter, where light and climate have their key and fader; its legend carries today's low and high in whole degrees, from the readings so far, the rest of today's hourly forecast and the daily forecast (`2°–8°`; `-3° to -1°` below zero, so a dash never meets a minus). The screen leads with the temperature under the sky's pictogram (sun, moon by night, cloud, fog, rain, snow, storm, wind), lit in the figure's ink. The two meta lines take, most urgent first: a warning, behind the marker (poor air over US AQI 100, strong gusts from 50 km/h, UV from very high; `Strong gusts`, its speed spoken in full); else rain or snow in the next 12 hours (`70% rain at 14:00`), or when falling rain stops (`Rain until 16:00`); else the condition. Then how it feels (`Feels 4°`). More than one warning shows the most urgent with a count of the rest (`Hazardous air +2`), or under 260 px the count alone (`3 warnings`), and is spoken in full. These warnings are about the reading, so they stay on the screen. Under 240 px the screen keeps the figure and one meta line, and the pictogram goes. Compact: glyph, place, screen; a row has no meta lines, so the first one takes the area's line under the name, a warning behind its marker (weather has no area of its own).
- **Panel:** how it feels on the screen's second channel. Then every warning by name and the sky in words (`Partly cloudy · 70% rain at 14:00`); today's temperatures with their low and high; the next eight hours and the next five days on strips of screen (the hour, `14` or `2PM` on the dashboard's hour cycle, or the day, the sky's pictogram, the temperature or high over low, a chance of rain from 20%), each spoken as one description; then every reading the entity has, printed on the plate under **Now** (dew point, humidity, wind with its direction and gusts, air quality, UV, pressure), a warning's reading behind the marker; and when it last updated. Without a forecast yet, **Next hours** says so.
- **Unavailable:** blank segments, an unlit cloud, "Unavailable" and when it was last seen, and the hours it was gone dashed on the meter; today's low and high from what was read stay. The panel draws no forecast, sky or readings. A weather that has no temperature yet says "No reading yet" over blank segments, as every reading does before its first value.

## Toggle

- **Module:** a mode you switch on and off (Do Not Disturb, Quiet, Guest), one `input_boolean` or `switch`. One key across the row, carrying the mode's own glyph (a bell struck through, a moon, guests, a suitcase, waves; power when it has none) and its state word, the way an on/off lamp's key does; its LED is the on signal. The screen says `ON` or `OFF` beside the mode's pictogram, lit in the figure's ink while on: a mode sets no parameter, so nothing on it wears a parameter's colour. The meta lines say until when it stays on, then since when (`Until 12:54`, `Since 11:02`), or since when alone. Compact: the square key, name and area, screen; the key's glyph tells a column of modes apart.
- **Panel:** when it turns itself off on the screen's second channel, behind a clock (`12:54`, "Turns off"), while it is on for a while; otherwise the module's meta lines. Then the key; what the mode does, in a sentence; and, given `onTurnOnFor`, a key per way to turn it on (30 min, 1 hour, Morning, Until off), what each ends at printed under it, under **Turn on for**, or **Keep on** while it is on, with the one it was turned on with chosen. Home Assistant has no timed-on service for either domain, so the host owns the timer, as on orb's tile. Four keys in a row, two by two on a phone. Today, neutral, with how long it has been on; when it last changed, and whether it is a switch or a toggle helper.
- **Unavailable:** blank segments, "Unavailable" and when it was last seen; the key lies flat with no state word, the panel's keys lie flat, and the hours it was gone are dashed on the meter.

## Humidifier

- **Module:** one `humidifier.*` entity, a humidifier or, by its `device_class`, a dehumidifier. The key switches it and its LED is the on signal; its glyph is mist rising from the unit, or a dehumidifier's grille over its tank, so a column of them tells the two apart. The screen leads with the room's humidity (`038%`) beside a drop, filled to the target and lit in the target's colour (orange, the module's one parameter) while on; a unit with no target lights it in the figure's ink. The meta lines say what the target does, then what the unit is doing: `Humidify to 45%` or `Dry to 50%`, then Humidifying, Drying or Idle (the mode when it reports no action).
